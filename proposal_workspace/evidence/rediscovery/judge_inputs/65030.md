## List B

- **1. `twapEMA` reads the EMAs in the wrong order, so the liquidation price uses the wrong blend of averages**
  - Root cause: line 816, `(int256 eonsEMA, int256 slowEMA, int256 fastEMA, , ) = oraclePack.getEMAs();`. `OraclePackLibrary.getEMAs` returns `(_spotEMA, _fastEMA, _slowEMA, _eonsEMA, _medianTick)`. So the local `eonsEMA` holds the spot EMA (120 s period), the local `slowEMA` holds the fast EMA (240 s), and the local `fastEMA` holds the slow EMA (600 s). The real eons EMA (1800 s) is thrown away. Line 817, `(6 * fastEMA + 3 * slowEMA + eonsEMA) / 10`, therefore computes 60% slow + 30% fast + 10% spot. The NatSpec says it should be 60% fast + 30% slow + 10% eons.
  - Attack sketch: `PanopticPool.getTWAP()` feeds this value into `dispatchFrom` as `twapTick`. That tick is one of the four solvency check ticks for liquidation. It is also the price used for the liquidation bonus in `_liquidate`, for `_forceExercise`, and for `_settlePremium`, and it is the reference for the `MAX_TWAP_DELTA_DISPATCH` (513 tick) staleness check. The value is meant to be the most smoothed price in the system. Instead it drops the 30-minute EMA completely and adds the near-instant spot EMA, which can move 75% of a clamped 149-tick step in one 64 s epoch. An attacker moves the Uniswap price and holds it for a few epochs. In each new epoch the attacker touches the pool (for example with a small mint) so that a clamped observation is written. Because no 30-minute term holds the price back, the computed TWAP follows the moved price fully after about 10 to 15 minutes. The 4 other liquidation ticks follow even faster. So an account that is healthy at the true long-run price can be pushed into "insolvent at all four ticks" and liquidated, with the bonus and the haircut priced at the moved tick. The same wrong value also changes force-exercise and premium-settlement prices for every call, without any attacker action.
  - Severity: Medium
  - Confidence: high that the order is wrong. Medium that the change in manipulation cost is large enough to be exploited for profit.

- **2. Division by 10 rounds toward zero, so the TWAP is biased upward by one tick for negative prices**
  - Root cause: line 817, `/ 10` on a signed `int256`. Solidity truncates toward zero. For a negative weighted sum this rounds up (for example -15/10 gives -1, not -2). Positive sums round down. So the TWAP is not rounded consistently toward negative infinity, as Uniswap tick math expects.
  - Attack sketch: in pools whose price sits at a negative tick, the liquidation and force-exercise price is always rounded one tick toward zero. A liquidator or a force exerciser who picks accounts right at the margin edge can choose cases where the one-tick shift flips the result of the solvency check at `twapTick`, or slightly changes the bonus they receive. The effect is at most one tick per call.
  - Severity: Medium (likely Low in practice)
  - Confidence: low

- **3. `twapEMA` reads only stored EMAs and has no idea how old they are, so the liquidation price can be stale**
  - Root cause: lines 816 and 817 use only the EMA fields in the `oraclePack` argument and ignore the epoch field. `PanopticPool.getTWAP()` passes the stored `s_oraclePack`. In `dispatchFrom` the updated pack from `getOracleTicks` is thrown away, so the pending observation for the current epoch never reaches the TWAP.
  - Attack sketch: after a quiet period with no pool interaction, the EMAs still show the old price. An attacker calls `dispatchFrom` right after a real price move and before anyone else touches the pool. `twapTick`, `spotTick` and `latestTick` all come from the same stale pack, so 3 of the 4 solvency check ticks reflect the old price. The attacker can liquidate or force-exercise an account at a price that the EMAs would not show after one update. In the other direction, honest force-exercise and premium-settlement calls revert with `StaleOracle` until someone updates the oracle.
  - Severity: Medium
  - Confidence: low
