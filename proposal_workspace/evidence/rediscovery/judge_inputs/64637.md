## List A

- **Any outsider can cancel an active bet before expiry, letting a losing bettor escape settlement.** Root cause: `cancel()` checks only stored completion status at lines 168–169 and rejects active cancellation only when `msg.sender == b.maker` at lines 173–174. It never requires expiry or authorizes other callers. Attack: after both parties fund an active bet, the losing taker calls `cancel()` before the judge resolves it; a losing maker can do the same through another address or helper contract. Lines 193–197 refund the stakes and permanently mark the bet cancelled, preventing resolution and depriving the winner of the opposing stake. **Severity: High. Confidence: high.**

## List B

- **1. Anyone can cancel a bet at any time, so the losing party can cancel an active bet and take back its stake before the judge resolves it**
  - Root cause: `cancel` reads the stored status `b.status` (line 165, checks at lines 168 and 173) and never calls `_status(b)`. It never checks `block.timestamp` against `b.acceptBy` or `b.resolveBy`. The stored status is never `EXPIRED`. So the only checks are `b.status >= IBet.Status.RESOLVED` (line 168) and `b.maker == msg.sender && b.status != IBet.Status.PENDING` (line 173). Any caller other than the maker can cancel a PENDING or ACTIVE bet at any moment, whether or not it has expired. The maker check at line 173 is also weak: the maker can call from a second address. This goes against the NatSpec at line 163, which says that only expired bets can be cancelled by anybody.
  - Attack sketch: Maker and taker each stake 100 and the bet is ACTIVE, well before `resolveBy`. The real-world outcome makes the taker the loser. The taker calls `cancel()` before the judge calls `resolve(maker)`, or front-runs the judge's `resolve` transaction in the mempool. The funds are withdrawn from Aave, if a pool is set, and both stakes are refunded. Status becomes CANCELLED, so `resolve` now reverts. The maker loses the 100 it had won. The maker can do the same from another address when the maker is losing. A second impact comes from the same root cause. Any third party can cancel every PENDING bet, for example by front-running the taker's `accept()`, or any ACTIVE bet. This blocks the core accept and resolve flow for the whole protocol.
  - Severity: High
  - Confidence: high

- **2. Status is written after the external transfers, so a maker that receives a token callback can re-enter `cancel` and drain the taker's stake (bets with no Aave pool)**
  - Root cause: `_bet.status = IBet.Status.CANCELLED` (line 197) is written only after `IERC20(b.asset).transfer(b.maker, makerRefund)` (line 193) and the taker transfer (line 194). There is no reentrancy guard. During a re-entrant call the stored status is still PENDING or ACTIVE, so the checks at lines 168 to 175 pass again. When `_aavePool` is zero, `makerRefund` is the full `b.makerStake` again, paid from the taker's deposit.
  - Attack sketch: The asset is a token that calls the recipient on transfer, such as an ERC777-style token. The maker is a contract, and the bet has no pool and is ACTIVE with stakes 100 and 100. A helper contract, which is not the maker so the line 173 check passes, calls `cancel()`. Line 193 sends 100 to the maker. The maker's receive hook calls the helper, and the helper calls `cancel()` again. The inner call sends another 100 to the maker. The inner taker transfer fails and is swallowed by the `catch`, and the inner call sets the status to CANCELLED. Back in the outer call, the taker transfer at line 194 also fails and is swallowed. The maker ends with 200 and the taker gets nothing. With an Aave pool the gain is limited, because the inner `withdraw` of a zero aToken balance reverts.
  - Severity: Medium
  - Confidence: low

- **3. Failed refunds are silently swallowed and the bet is still marked CANCELLED, so those funds are locked in the contract for good**
  - Root cause: lines 193 and 194 wrap each transfer in `try ... {} catch {}`. They also ignore the boolean that `transfer` returns. Line 197 then sets CANCELLED no matter what happened. No other function can move funds once the status is CANCELLED: `cancel` reverts at line 168, and `resolve` needs ACTIVE.
  - Attack sketch: Any caller can cancel a bet whose maker or taker cannot receive the token at that moment. Examples are an address blacklisted by USDC or USDT, or a token that returns `false` instead of reverting. That party's refund stays in the bet contract forever. The money was already withdrawn from Aave at line 185, so it does not even earn interest. An out-of-gas attack on the `try` calls, where the caller supplies just enough gas for the outer call to finish, does not look practical for standard tokens. The 1/64 of gas kept back after a failed call is too small to pay for the storage write at line 197.
  - Severity: Medium
  - Confidence: low

- **4. Aave interest and any amount above the two stakes stays in the contract with no way out**
  - Root cause: lines 183 to 188 withdraw the whole `aTokenBalance`. Lines 187 and 188 then cap the refunds at `b.makerStake` and `b.takerStake`. Unlike `resolve` (lines 152 to 156), `cancel` does not send the leftover balance to `_treasury`. After CANCELLED nothing can move it. On a PENDING bet with a pool, `takerRefund` at line 188 also pays the maker's interest, up to `takerStake`, to a taker who never deposited.
  - Attack sketch: Anyone cancels a long-running ACTIVE bet with a pool, which is possible at any time because of candidate 1. All accrued interest is withdrawn and then left in the bet contract for good, so the treasury loses it. On a PENDING pool bet, the taker, or anyone, calls `cancel()`. The taker receives the maker's accrued interest even though the taker staked nothing.
  - Severity: Medium
  - Confidence: low
