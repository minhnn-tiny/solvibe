- **Overwritten credit balances permit liquidation of solvent accounts.** Root cause: `credits = ...` at `target.sol:1336–1338` replaces earlier same-token credit amounts instead of accumulating them; only the last qualifying leg contributes collateral. ## List B

1. **Credit amount is overwritten instead of summed, so a position with two or more credit legs in the same token under-counts the owner's collateral**
   - **Root cause:** inside the leg loop, the branch `if (tokenId.width(index) == 0 && tokenId.isLong(index) == 1)` (target.sol lines 1329-1339) does `credits = tokenId.tokenType(index) == 0 ? amountsMoved.rightSlot() : amountsMoved.leftSlot();`. It uses `=` and not `+=`. When a tokenId has several zero-width long legs (credits) of the token being evaluated, only the last one is returned. `TokenId.validate` allows this, because it only requires the legs to sit at different chunks (different strike or tokenType). At mint, `CollateralTracker._updateBalancesAndSettle` burns the owner's shares for the full sum of all credit legs (each credit is a `longAmount`, so `tokenToPay` grows by every credit). But `_getMargin` adds back only `creditAmounts` (lines 1182-1183), which holds just one credit per tokenId and token. The owner's balance in the solvency check is therefore too low by the sum of every other credit leg.
   - **Attack sketch:** Alice holds one tokenId with legs like leg0 = short put partnered with leg1 = credit in token1 (a cash-secured put), and leg2 = short put at another strike partnered with leg3 = credit in token1. She could also hold two plain credit legs next to a loan leg. CollateralTracker1 burned shares worth C1 + C2 from her. `_getRequiredCollateralAtTickSinglePosition(..., false)` returns `credits = C2`, so `isAccountSolvent` sees a balance that is C1 too small. Bob, an unprivileged liquidator, waits until Alice's real margin is still positive but her reported margin is below the requirement at all four oracle ticks. He then calls `PanopticPool.dispatchFrom(..., account = Alice, positionIdListTo = Alice's list, positionIdListToFinal = [])`. `_liquidate` burns Alice's positions and pays Bob the liquidation bonus out of Alice's collateral, even though she was solvent. The same under-count also makes Alice's own mints, withdrawals and burns that end in a solvency check fail, even though she has enough collateral.
   - **Severity:** Medium (a solvent user can lose the liquidation bonus to a third party, and core operations are wrongly blocked; the loss needs the victim to hold a tokenId with two or more same-token credit legs).
   - **Confidence:** medium (the `=` versus `+=` defect is clear; the impact depends on users holding such positions).

2. **Credits returned by this function are counted twice when the account is liquidated, which hides protocol loss**
   - **Root cause:** lines 1329-1339 report the credit amount as part of the owner's balance (through `_getTotalRequiredCollateral` at lines 1299-1300 and `_getMargin` at lines 1182-1183). During `_liquidate`, `getMargin` is called before the burn, so `tokenData0/1.rightSlot()` already includes the credit. Then `_burnAllOptionsFrom` closes the credit leg, and `settleBurn` returns `tokenToPay = -credit`, so `netPaid` also includes the credit as money that came back to the liquidatee. `getLiquidationBonus` computes `collateralRemaining = balance - (bonus + netPaid)` (lines 551-557 and 605-611). It removes `shortPremium` from the balance to avoid exactly this kind of double count, but it does not remove credits. So `collateralRemaining` is too high by the full credit amount in each token.
   - **Attack sketch:** The attacker controls two accounts. Account A sells options in a chunk. Account B buys long options in that chunk so that it owes a large long premium. B also opens a large credit leg with its own tokens, then lets its position become insolvent (or arranges this with an adverse price move). A colluding liquidator liquidates B. B's long premium is settled to the sellers, including A. `collateralRemaining` looks non-negative because the credit is counted twice. So `haircutPremia` does not claw back the premium, and the cross-token loss mitigation in `getLiquidationBonus` does not run. The real shortfall then shows up in `settleLiquidation` as a virtual-share shortfall. That shortfall is covered by minting shares (`_internalSupply += shortfall`), which takes value from the passive liquidity providers. A keeps the premium.
   - **Severity:** Medium (loss to liquidity providers, capped by the size of the credit legs and of the premium that can be routed).
   - **Confidence:** low (the double count follows from the code I read; whether it can be turned into a profitable attack depends on how the haircut and settlement handle other edge cases I did not fully trace).
