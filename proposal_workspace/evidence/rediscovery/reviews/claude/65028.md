- **Liquidation reverts on a checked underflow when the account fails the cross-buffer solvency test but its raw cross balance still covers its requirement**
  - Root cause: line 521, `Math.min(balanceCross / 2, thresholdCross - balanceCross)`, runs in checked arithmetic. The function assumes that an account being liquidated always has `balanceCross < thresholdCross`. That is no longer true. `isAccountSolvent` (target.sol lines 1004-1059) only credits the surplus of the other token after scaling it by `_crossBufferRatio` (at most `CROSS_BUFFER`, and 0 once pool utilization is above 90%). `getCrossBalances` (lines 515-519) counts the full surplus. The two use the same `tokenData` and the same TWAP tick (`PanopticPool._liquidate` calls `getMargin` at `twapTick` with the same premium flags as the insolvency check in `dispatchFrom`). So an account can be insolvent at all four ticks while `balanceCross >= thresholdCross`. The subtraction then panics and the whole liquidation reverts.
  - Attack sketch: the attacker opens positions so that their account is short in token0 and has a large surplus in token1. They can mint a small position while vault utilization is high, so the stored global utilization makes the cross-buffer ratio 0. When the price moves, the account becomes insolvent in token0. Every liquidator who calls `dispatchFrom(..., positionIdListToFinal = [])` gets a revert inside `getLiquidationBonus`. Settle and force exercise are also blocked, because those paths need the account to be solvent at all ticks. The account stays open until it is insolvent on the raw cross balance too. At that point the bonus is close to zero, so liquidators have little reason to act, and a fast price move turns the delayed liquidation into protocol loss for the PLPs.
  - Severity: Medium
  - Confidence: medium-high

- **Credit legs are counted twice, so the protocol-loss estimate is too low and both loss-mitigation steps are skipped**
  - Root cause: lines 551-554. The code removes `shortPremium` from `tokenData0/1.rightSlot()` because that premium shows up again in `netPaid`. It does not remove the credit amounts. `_getMargin` (target.sol lines 1185-1186) adds `creditAmounts` (width-0 long legs) to the balance. When those legs are burned, `CollateralTracker._updateBalancesAndSettle` returns the credit to the user as a negative `tokenToPay`. That negative amount is inside `netPaid`. So `balance0 - paid0` (line 609) is too high by the credit amount. With that error, the check at line 564 or line 582 (`paid0 > balance0`) can fail even though the account really is short of token0. The returned `collateralRemaining` also looks non-negative, so `haircutPremia` treats `collateralDelta = 0` and claws back no long premium.
  - Attack sketch: the attacker's account A opens a token0 credit leg of size X (the credit costs nothing, because it is paid back on burn). A also holds long legs in a range where the attacker's account B is the short seller. The attacker pushes up the premium A owes to B, and A's other legs lose token0 until A is insolvent with a real token0 shortfall L ≤ X. A also keeps surplus token1. The attacker, or anyone, liquidates A. `getLiquidationBonus` reports no token0 loss, so A's token1 surplus is not converted to cover it, and `haircutPremia` does not revoke the premium paid to B. `settleLiquidation` then mints the L shortfall to vault0, so the PLPs pay. A keeps its leftover token1 shares, and B keeps the premium. This is the collusion case that the comment in `_liquidate` says the haircut is meant to stop.
  - Severity: High (Medium if credits are rarely combined with long premium)
  - Confidence: medium

- **The commission burned during the liquidation burn is missing from `netPaid`, so `collateralRemaining` is too high by that fee**
  - Root cause: lines 557-558 and 609-612 assume that `netPaid` is everything the liquidatee pays. `CollateralTracker.settleBurn` burns `commissionFee` shares from the option owner after `_updateBalancesAndSettle` has already computed `tokenPaid`. The fee is never added to the returned `paid` amount. So `paid0` and `paid1` are too low, and the protocol loss (`balance - paid`) is understated by the commission in each token.
  - Attack sketch: when the liquidatee's account is near zero after the burn, the missed commission turns a small real shortfall into a reported surplus. No token conversion happens and no premium is haircut. The vault absorbs the difference when `settleLiquidation` finds the liquidatee's virtual shares short. The amount is small per liquidation, because it is a notional or premium fee.
  - Severity: Medium (likely Low in practice)
  - Confidence: low

- **The bonus comes from the balance before the burn and is not capped by what the liquidatee has left afterwards, so self-liquidation pays the liquidator out of PLP funds**
  - Root cause: line 521 sets the bonus to as much as `balanceCross / 2` of the balance before the burn. Lines 562-599 only move value between the two tokens. They never cut the bonus when `paid0 > balance0` and `paid1 > balance1` both hold. `settleLiquidation` then mints the unpaid part of the bonus to the liquidator from vault shares.
  - Attack sketch: the attacker funds account A, opens positions that close at a large cost in both tokens, and waits until A is insolvent. The attacker then liquidates A from account C. C receives up to half of A's balance before the burn, even though the burn used up all of A's collateral. The vault mints the difference, so the PLPs pay. The profit is limited to the bonus minus what A lost to other parties, so this pays only when the attacker also holds the other side of A's burn costs.
  - Severity: Medium
  - Confidence: low
