## Strengths

- **The scope reduction is supported by evidence.** V37 reports RQ1a mean half-widths reaching 0.711, including 0.634 without unresolved outcomes in the corresponding condition. Withdrawing its precision promise is appropriate.
- **The replacement analysis has meaningful validation.** V37 tests 76 ordered author-rate vectors, four unresolved-outcome mechanisms and 24 finite frames. Saved results report worst error upper bounds of 0.0408 and marginal-coverage lower bounds of 0.9567 under the simulated settings.
- **The endpoint avoids a basic interpretive error.** Functional failure is reported separately; a smaller unconditional detected-vulnerability count cannot automatically be presented as greater security.
- **Missing outcomes remain consequential.** Adverse bounds remain primary, undefined denominators prevent directional conclusions, and incomplete adjudication cannot silently become a negative result.
- **Discovery asymmetry is addressed directly.** Audit-derived attacks and pooled replay cannot change locked primary labels. Executable attribution and independent adjudication make this more than an LLM evaluation wrapper.
- **The proposal distinguishes planning evidence from observation.** It does not claim that automated timing, candidate rediscovery, three development protocols or simulation establish human throughput or population recall.

## Weaknesses

1. **The human feasibility evidence needed to accept this tightly budgeted programme is still missing.**

   **Evidence:** `dev_case/commitment.md` contains blank dates, availability, staffing and funding fields; `dev_case/timesheet.csv` contains only its header. Section 8 acknowledges that the completed timed case is unavailable.

   The main reservation is approximately 880 of 900 available hours. Section 5.7 permits only 0.178 hours—10.68 minutes per implementation—for confirmation, correction, packaging and lock administration, shared across up to three candidates. That may prove workable with automation, but neither the existing automated timings nor candidate-matching results demonstrate it. The 1,800-hour annual plan also assumes no coursework or teaching, while native-agent compute remains unpriced.

   This is substantive because inadequate time can change construction success, unresolved outcomes and detection sensitivity simultaneously. The retained power region tolerates very little historical unresolvedness; an administrative cap alone does not establish that valid work fits it.

   **Required change:** Submit one completed human development evidence package containing the dated student commitment and a probability-selected, end-to-end timed case, including N functions, failures, actual eligible staff participation and measured resource use reconciled against the stated reservations; retain the remaining two cases as mandatory prelaunch checks.

## Questions for authors

**OQ1 — Does the proposal meet the bar apart from item 10, and what would the score become?**

Yes, for **weak acceptance of the revised, conditional research programme**. If the commitment and timed case credibly demonstrate valid work within the caps, I would give **Score: 4**. Merely filling in dates would not suffice.

I accept one completed case before acceptance and the complete three-case record before launch, replacing my earlier request for all three before acceptance. These cases establish operational credibility, not precise construction or unresolved-outcome rates. Frame freezing, final simulation validation and funded staffing remain prelaunch requirements.

**OQ2.1 — Make RQ1b principal and RQ1a descriptive: approve with conditions.**

Retain the stated interpretation: a standardized comparison among each author’s working implementations, not a causal authorship effect or a comparison over an identical set of working functions. Preserve functional-success reporting and keep RQ1a descriptive. These conditions are already substantially present.

The revised claim must receive its own novelty assessment; it cannot inherit the earlier sampling-distortion claim. The comparison table above explains why I nevertheless consider it defensible.

**OQ2.2 — Reduce required power to 0.60 at exactly +0.15: approve with conditions.**

I accept this as an explicit resource–informativeness tradeoff for a proposal. It is not equivalent to the former 0.80 design. Retain the frozen-frame requirement that every relevant adjusted Monte Carlo lower bound exceed 0.60 inside the declared region, and do not interpret a nondirectional result as evidence of equivalence or negligible harm.

Even without unresolved outcomes, V37’s worst estimated power is 0.763, with lower bound 0.734. The revision therefore meaningfully reduces the promised informativeness.

**OQ2.3 — Replace rare-rate certification with conditional assumptions and a diagnostic: approve with conditions.**

The diagnostic must remain a warning, never evidence that the study lies inside the power region. V38 demonstrates why: at historical unresolvedness 0.065 with protocol-shared failures, its reported firing rate is only 0.053 in the main study and 0.012 in development.

Keep adverse bounds, report the complete fixed sample regardless of the diagnostic, and retain error/coverage checks outside the power region. Section 6.3 now makes these commitments. Conditional power remains conditional even when no warning fires.

**OQ2.4 — Replace the construction-yield confidence gate: approve with conditions.**

Three development V functions cannot certify a stratum-specific population yield. Descriptive reporting and an operational completion check are more defensible than such certification.

Retain stratum-specific counts, the prohibition on favorable replacements, and the distinction between legitimate construction failure and failure to supply the prescribed procedure. The pooled warning cannot establish V-stratum adequacy; the final-frame simulation and valid human execution must remain separate launch requirements.
