## Summary

Version 25 makes RQ1b the principal estimand. RQ1b is the detected-vulnerability rate among working implementations, agents minus historical code, standardized over the constructible strata. RQ1a, the flagged-only comparison, becomes descriptive.

The allocation is:
- 22 protocols, each with up to one V and six N functions;
- two attempts for each of Claude Code and Codex CLI.

The inference procedure was selected in V33 and is now frozen at Holm level 0.03 and interval multiplier 1.175. V37 validates it across 76 ordered vectors of unresolved-outcome rates by author and four ways of generating unresolved outcomes. The authors also make four requests:
- lower the power requirement to 0.60 at +0.15, required only inside a stated region of unresolved rates;
- replace certification of unresolved rates with a fixed diagnostic (V38);
- replace the construction-yield gate with a warning rule;
- rerun the full checks on the frozen frame before main sampling.

The student's dated commitment and timed case are still missing.

**What I checked.** I read these files:
- the proposal, my iteration-3 review, the rebuttal and the open questions;
- the scripts for V29, V30, V32, V33, V35, V37 and V38, and the V22 cost model (`tools/power_sim_v22.py`);
- the result files for V33, V34, V37 and V38;
- the strict frame counts (the lists in `tools/power_sim_v29.py`, taken from `evidence/frame_audit/strict/`);
- the rediscovery results (`evidence/rediscovery/RESULTS.md`), the timing record (`evidence/timings.md`), the development kit (`dev_case/`) and the pilot report.

Every number quoted from V33, V34, V37 and V38 matches its file. I recomputed the strict counts: 54 and 109 V functions, 3,620 N functions, medians 1, 4 and 92, and 10 and 1 audits with no V function.

I also ran the authors' own generator in memory, writing no files, to get three numbers the proposal does not report:
- the constructible V share of the domain;
- the agent and historical N rates implied by the +0.15 target;
- the RQ1b interval width.

They appear under weaknesses 5 to 7. I changed no file.

**My iteration-3 weaknesses, one line each.**
1. "Little difference" could never be reached. **Resolved.** The reading is removed and RQ1a is descriptive. Its precision is now worse (largest mean half-width 0.711 in V37), which leads to new weakness 8.
2. D mixed functional failure with security. **Resolved.** The principal estimand is now conditional security, with an exact decomposition in 6.2; I checked the identity. Conditioning on working outputs raises a question the simulation does not test (new weakness 7).
3. The fixed minimum broke its own reservation rule. **Resolved in the arithmetic.** 22 × 39.98 = 880 ≤ 900, and 3 × 39.98 = 120 ≤ 150. But it fits because planning averages were turned into hard limits for each implementation, which causes new weaknesses 1 and 2. Poor V construction now barely moves RQ1b, because V is 1–3% of the domain.
4. The V distribution was not measured. **Resolved.** A random order of 25 audits was recounted strictly, and the simulated frames are resampled from it. The reading was done by agents, and the remaining eligibility checks are stated.
5. No rule fixed the retained scenarios. **Resolved as a procedure.** There is one Holm level and one multiplier. Error and coverage are checked on all 76 vectors, power only inside the region, and the whole check is rerun on the frozen frame. Whether the region is realistic is the remaining problem (weakness 3).
6. Cut candidate lists hid a loss of sensitivity. **Resolved.** Cuts are recorded, and a secondary bound treats them as unresolved (5.7 and the table in 6.2).

Iteration-3 questions 3, 4, 5 and 6 are answered. Question 1 (a subset with fixes dated after the model cutoffs) is deferred to frame freezing. Question 2 is partly answered by the check-revision rule in 5.5.

## Strengths

1. **The inference is validated to an unusual standard.** In V37 the worst error upper bound is 0.0408 and the worst coverage lower bound is 0.9567, under all four ways of generating unresolved outcomes. This holds at all 76 vectors, including those far outside the power region, such as a historical rate of 0.065. Error control therefore does not rest on the operating assumptions. Only power does.
2. **The reporting is honest.** Every number matches its file, and failures are reported:
   - the 20-protocol design's lower bound of 0.575;
   - the dropped corner at 0.598 and 0.606;
   - V34's loss of power;
   - the diagnostic's weakness for failures that affect a whole protocol.
3. **The frame is now measured.** The strict recount separates colliding function names and applies the same eligibility rule to V and N.
4. **The final check on the frozen frame has a fixed scope.** Replacement calibration uses a separate stream and fresh validation, with no fallback. Failure means no launch.
5. **Development is a fixed probability sample.** No case is replaced and no extension is triggered by an outcome. The kit implements the draw and includes a self-check (`dev_case/draw_protocols.py`).
6. **The closing rules are sound.** They separate a completed disagreement between adjudicators from an inability to assess, and only the first can become "not confirmed".

## Weaknesses

1. **The student's confirmation limit is the planning model's average confirmation time per implementation, not a time per candidate.**
   - **Problem.** Sections 5.7 and 8 give each scheduled implementation 0.178 hours for all candidate confirmation, body correction, packaging and lock administration, for up to three candidates. Time cannot move between implementations. In the cost model that produced this number, 0.178 is the middle candidate rate: the expected number of candidates per implementation. Confirmation is priced at 1.0 hour per candidate. So 0.178 hours is an average over all implementations, most of which have no candidate.
   - **Evidence.** `tools/power_sim_v22.py`, line 21, has middle rates `(9, 2.25, 0.15, 0.178, 1.0, 1.875)`, and the cost term is `op + cand * conf`. An implementation with one candidate therefore gets 10.7 minutes for it, against the model's 60 minutes (30 at the low rate). With three candidates, each gets 3.6 minutes. For comparison, the automated agent needed 23 minutes for the first 63008 package, starting from prepared pilot material (`evidence/timings.md`).
   - **Consequences.**
     - A candidate cut by the limit is "not confirmed", not unresolved (table in 6.2). The loss therefore does not appear in the adverse bounds. It lowers recall, which V37 assumes is 0.5 to 0.8.
     - Development will not catch it. A cut is a legitimate recorded outcome, not a failure to supply the procedure (section 8, step 5).
     - If audit-grade historical flaws take longer to confirm than agent flaws [uncertain], the limit lowers historical recall more than agent recall. That pushes the detected difference toward "agents worse" with no security difference behind it.
   - **Required change:** Give each candidate its own confirmation limit at the planning time (1.0 hour, at most three per implementation). Provision the total at the high pilot candidate rate of 3/13 and recheck the 900-hour bound. By the section 8 formula, that costs about 920 hours for the main allocation, so the bound needs about 20 more hours or one fewer protocol.

2. **The adjudication reservation is also fixed per implementation, and it is several times smaller than the proposal's own time per package.**
   - **Problem.** Section 8 divides each source's adjudication hours by the maximum number of scheduled implementations that can produce packages. All packages from one implementation share that reservation. Review that does not finish leaves the outcome unresolved (6.2). The main study schedules up to 22 × 7 × 5 = 770 implementations. Even if all 121 hours of each adjudicator went to the main study, each implementation would get 0.157 hours, or 9.4 minutes, for up to three packages.
   - **Evidence.** Section 8 says: "For each adjudicator, the sum cannot exceed 121 hours. Within each source, divide reserved hours by the maximum number of scheduled implementations…". It also says: "The earlier 0.5-hour-per-package figure remains a workload assumption only."
   - **Consequence.** At the +0.15 target, the agents' detected N rate is 0.18 to 0.25 across the 24 frames (my run of the generator). That means more than 100 agent packages among about 616 agent implementations. If most of them cannot be judged in 9 minutes, the agent unresolved rate lands far above the region's limit of 0.013. V37 shows what such rates do: at a historical rate of 0.065 alone, power is 0.22. The development diagnostic may then stop launch in month two, after the student's month is spent.
   - **Required change:** Replace the reservation per implementation with a fixed limit per package of 0.5 hours for each adjudicator, the same for every author. Provision the total at the high candidate rate and check it against the 121-hour ceiling before development.

3. **None of V37's four unresolved-outcome mechanisms is the one this procedure produces. So the operating region and the diagnostic are stated in the wrong units.**
   - **Problem.** In V37, unresolved status never depends on whether an implementation has a package. It is drawn per implementation, per function, per protocol, or from a function score. Under the 6.2 rules, the two unresolved sources that the time limits drive both need a package: an adjudicator cannot assess, or review time ends. So unresolved outcomes should grow with the number of detected flaws.
   - **What the simulation misses.**
     - Under +0.15, agent packages in N are three to seven times as common as historical ones. The agent unresolved rate therefore rises with the effect.
     - For a positive claim, an unresolved agent package counts as not vulnerable. It removes exactly the evidence the claim needs.
     - An unresolved historical package counts as vulnerable, which is close to its true state.
   - **Evidence on the diagnostic.** The diagnostic divides a count of protocols by m_a, the largest number of one author's outcomes in a protocol (7 historical, 14 per configuration). In development, it fires at most 0.018 inside the region. Just outside the region it fires 0.014 at (0, 0, 0.013) and 0.031 at (0, 0, 0.02) (`tools/power_results_v38.txt`). It cannot tell the two cases apart.
   - **Required change:** Add a fifth mechanism to the final-frame check, in which unresolved status arises only among implementations with a package, at a rate per package for each author. Restate the retained region and the development check as unresolved shares per package.

4. **The recall that the power claim assumes is not checked under the planned limits.**
   - **Problem.** The +0.15 target is a difference in detected rates, and the generator sets recall at 0.5, 0.7 or 0.8 (`RECALLS` in `tools/power_sim_v29.py`).
   - **Evidence.** The only recall evidence matches candidate lists: 17 of 17 and 13 of 17, with the function named, and no PoV, correction or adjudication. `evidence/rediscovery/RESULTS.md` itself calls these upper bounds on confirmed rediscovery. The gate in 5.9 counts "rediscoveries" but does not require confirmation and adjudication within the main study's limits.
   - **Required change:** State in 5.9 that a gate rediscovery counts only if it is confirmed by execution and passes both adjudications within the main study's limits per candidate and per package. Passing the gate then supports recall near the 0.5 floor used in V37.

5. **The +0.15 target is a three- to seven-fold difference on ordinary functions, and the proposal does not say so.**
   - **Problem.** In the 24 frames, the constructible V share is 0.011 to 0.031. The strict counts give 54/3,674 = 0.015 and 109/3,729 = 0.029. So RQ1b is 97–99% a comparison on N functions.
   - **Evidence.**
     - In my run of the generator, the historical detected N rate is 0.024 to 0.083.
     - At +0.15, the agents' detected N rate is 0.18 to 0.25, a ratio of 2.9 to 7.4. Their underlying N vulnerability rate is 0.22 to 0.50.
     - Worst-frame power at +0.10 is 0.395.
     - Negative targets are −0.011 to −0.045, with power near zero, so "agents safer" cannot be shown.
     - Verified studies with executable or matched measurement report smaller ratios. Mahmud et al. find about two-fold more confirmed runtime violations in AI-written C++ (https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00107). Shaw finds 3.2 to 3.9-fold, but with static scanners (https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28021).
   - **Required change:** In sections 3 and 6.3, add a table giving, for each frame, the historical and agent detected N rates at +0.15 and their ratio. Report power at the target that doubles each frame's historical detected N rate.

6. **A null RQ1b has no pre-specified reading, and the main-study diagnostic only withdraws a power statement.**
   - **Problem.** The proposal says a broad null "will remain inconclusive", and a diagnostic flag "withdraws the planned power statement". Once data exist, the realized interval is the precision statement. Withdrawing a statement made before collection changes no inference. The expected RQ1b interval width is never reported.
   - **Evidence.** I ran the generator for 300 studies per frame on eight frames, with unresolved status drawn independently per implementation.
     - With no unresolved outcomes, the mean half-width of the full interval was 0.055–0.081 at the exact null and 0.087–0.103 at +0.15.
     - At the exact null, the upper limit fell below +0.10 in 0.60–0.98 of studies with no unresolved outcomes, and in 0.39–0.85 at (0.013, 0.013, 0.0075).
     - At a true +0.05, the upper limit fell below +0.10 in 0.08–0.30 of studies.

     So a null study can often rule out a large excess of agent flaws. V37 already checks the coverage this reading needs, because exact +0.10 is one of its conditions.
   - **Required change:** Replace the rule that withdraws the power statement with a pre-specified reading of the RQ1b upper limit. The reading should give the largest agent excess consistent with the data, and say whether it is below a stated margin such as +0.10, under the same multiplicity rule as directional claims. Report its simulated distribution for each frame in 6.3.

7. **The principal estimand conditions on working outputs, but the generator makes working independent of vulnerability and lets historical code always work.**
   - **Problem.** Section 6.2 itself says working outputs "can have different function mixtures across authors". In the generator, every agent attempt works with probability 0.72 regardless of the function, and historical implementations have no working draw at all (`draw` in `tools/power_sim_v37.py`). If agents fail more often on harder functions, which are also more often vulnerable, their conditional rate falls for reasons unrelated to security on any given function. The pilot saw this: in two runs, Aider "wrote the bug but broke the function" (`docs/pilot/pilot_results.md`, item 3). The validity of inference for the stated estimand is not at risk. Its meaning and its power are.
   - **Required change:** Pre-specify a secondary estimate restricted to functions where the historical implementation works and at least one attempt of the configuration works, with equal weight per function. Add one generator setting to the final-frame check in which the agent working probability falls with the function score and the historical working rate is below one.

8. **RQ1a stays listed as a research question, but at this allocation its interval is too wide to say anything.**
   - **Evidence.** V37's largest mean RQ1a half-width is 0.711, or 0.634 with no unresolved outcome, with an uncalibrated multiplier. The V-only comparison rests on at most 22 functions. The selection effect that motivates the project in section 2 is therefore not estimated.
   - **Required change:** Remove RQ1a from the paper-1 research questions in 6.1. Report the V-only estimates as a descriptive table with sample sizes and no interval.

9. **No human evidence exists yet.**
   - **Evidence.** `dev_case/commitment.md` is blank, and `dev_case/timesheet.csv` contains only its header. Every limit in section 8 equals a V22 middle planning rate, not a measurement.
   - **Required change:** Supply the dated commitment and the first timed case. Record the time spent per candidate on confirmation and per package on adjudication, so that weaknesses 1 and 2 can be checked against data.

## Ratings

- C1: concern — With V at 1–3% of the domain, the principal result is an agent-versus-developer comparison on Solidity functions the audit did not flag. The selection effect that motivated the project is no longer estimated, so novelty rests on the measurement design.
- C2: pass — Contracts hold funds, developers report daily AI use, and the proposal claims no rate of AI-caused losses.
- C3: pass — Blind campaigns for each implementation, attribution confined to the function body, estimated domain shares and inference from bounds go well beyond applying an LLM to a task.
- C4: concern — The limits per implementation for confirmation and adjudication contradict the proposal's own planning times per candidate and per package, and the recall and unresolved assumptions behind the power claim depend on them (weaknesses 1–3).
- C5: pass — The attacker, environment, recorded assumptions for each protocol, severity rubric and exclusions are explicit.
- C6: concern — Error and coverage are validated very well, but the target is a three- to seven-fold difference, a null has no reading, and recall and unresolved outcomes linked to packages are not checked (weaknesses 3–6).
- C7: concern — The budget arithmetic now holds only because averages became limits; correcting the limits costs about 20 hours, and no human timing or dated commitment exists (weaknesses 1, 2 and 9).
- C8: pass — Attacks run offline, new findings go to security contacts with a 90-day re-check and an embargo, and seeds stay local and marked.

## Questions for authors

1. Was the 0.178-hour value meant as a limit for each implementation, given that V22 uses it as a candidate rate times 1.0 hour?
2. During confirmation, may an LLM agent build the PoV under student supervision, as for the 63008 package? If so, which part of the limit is human time, and how is that agent kept away from audit material?
3. How many adjudicator hours does each source receive: development, main study, duplicate requests, seeds, controls and audit-assisted work?
4. The strict counts give a between-audit standard deviation of about 0.58 on the logit scale for V rates (recommendation-inclusive). The observed-fix counts give 0.91, though their zeros partly reflect private fixes. The generator uses 0.5 for the protocol effect. Will the final-frame check include a larger value?
5. Reviewers may recall published findings for historical V code. Is the recall flag in 6.8 also applied to reviewer candidate lists?

## Open questions

**OQ1.** Apart from item 10, the proposal does not yet meet the bar for a 4, because of weaknesses 1 and 2. If item 10 showed the limits workable, I would give 4. "Workable" here means that candidates were confirmed and packages adjudicated within the limits for each implementation, not only that each stage stopped on time. Fixing the limits as in weaknesses 1 and 2, and adding the null reading in weakness 6, would also earn a 4. The timed case would then remain a launch condition.

**OQ2.**
1. **Principal estimand: approve, with a condition.** V27 and V37 show that no examined allocation gives RQ1a useful precision. The condition is that the proposal states that RQ1b is 97–99% an N-stratum comparison and drops RQ1a as a research question (weaknesses 5 and 8).
2. **Power of 0.60 at +0.15: approve, with a condition.** The requirement applies to the worst frame. Median-frame power in V33 is 0.89 with no unresolved outcomes. The condition is that the proposal reports the implied ratios (weakness 5) and pre-specifies the upper-limit reading (weakness 6), so that a study without a directional claim still has a stated result.
3. **Unresolved-outcome readiness: approve the removal of certification, with a condition.** Certification could not work: no unresolved outcome among 216 gives an upper 80% limit of 0.0075 at best, as the V33 script's own description states. Adverse bounds stay primary. The condition is that the region and the development check are restated in units per package, with a mechanism linked to packages (weakness 3). The rule that withdraws the power statement should be replaced by the upper-limit reading.
4. **Construction yield: approve.** Three cases contain at most three V functions, so they cannot certify a yield. Because V is 1–3% of the domain, poor V construction barely moves RQ1b. It matters for RQ1a and for audit-assisted work. V and N yields should be reported separately, and the proposal should keep stating that the pooled warning cannot detect poor V construction.

## Overall score

The revision does what I asked in iteration 3:
- it separates security from functional failure;
- its allocation fits the reservation rule;
- it rests on a measured V distribution.

The validation of the inference is the most careful I have reviewed.

My score stays at 3 because of one specific error with large effects. Average times were turned into hard limits for each implementation, for both confirmation and adjudication. Under those limits, recall and unresolved rates would probably leave the simulated assumptions, and development would not catch the loss of recall.

These changes would raise my score to 4:
- limits per candidate and per package, provisioned at the high candidate rate (weaknesses 1 and 2);
- a mechanism for unresolved outcomes linked to packages (weakness 3);
- a pre-specified reading of a null RQ1b (weakness 6).

Score: 3
