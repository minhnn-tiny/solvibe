## Summary

The proposal compares agent-generated Solidity function bodies with historical implementations using probability sampling, audit-blind vulnerability discovery, executable attacks, and body-only corrections. Its principal estimand is a standardized difference in detected vulnerability among working implementations; functional failures and unresolved outcomes remain explicit.

This is a credible empirical research direction with substantial methodological care. However, acceptance is premature. Human feasibility remains unmeasured, the simulation validation omits important working-status mechanisms, and the extremely short confirmation allowance may make the principal result depend heavily on which vulnerabilities are easiest to confirm.

## Strengths

- The proposal correctly distinguishes historical code from verified human-only code, detected vulnerability from latent vulnerability, and conditional comparisons from causal effects.
- Including unflagged functions addresses a substantive limitation of benchmarks selected around historical failures.
- Own-campaign discovery, locking before audit access, executable attribution, and independent adjudication provide a stronger evidential basis than static-warning counts.
- The threat model specifies attacker privileges, capital, reachable states, ordering assumptions, and protected entitlements. The worked example explicitly tests an alternative explanation involving H-02.
- Adverse bounds, fixed sampling, retained failures, and restrictions on reallocating effort reduce opportunities for outcome-dependent analysis.
- The protected year excludes the repair extension, and the disclosure policy appropriately considers continuing deployed exposure.

## Weaknesses

1. **The proposed workload has not been demonstrated by the people who must execute it.**

   **Problem:** Feasibility depends on unusually tight human allowances and several funded roles. Automated execution time cannot establish that a first-year student can construct, confirm, document, and coordinate this study within twelve months.

   **Evidence:** Section 8 allocates 0.178 hours—10.68 minutes—per implementation for candidate confirmation, a body-only correction, packaging, and lock administration. It explicitly calls these caps rather than measured adequate effort. `dev_case/commitment.md` contains unfilled fields and an unsigned commitment; `dev_case/timesheet.csv` contains only its header. Named staffing, source reservations, and native-agent compute costs also remain unsettled.

   **Required change:** Submit the completed student development dossier specified in section 8, including the dated commitment and one probability-selected case performed with the actual prescribed roles and reservations, documenting human time, failures, and incomplete decisions.

2. **The inference simulations do not adequately exercise the conditioning mechanism central to RQ1b.**

   **Problem:** Validation over many unresolved-rate vectors does not establish robustness to heterogeneous working probabilities. These probabilities determine the denominators and which functions contribute to the principal comparison.

   **Evidence:** In `tools/power_sim_v37.py`, `draw()` generates agent working status using independent Bernoulli draws with probabilities 0.9 and 0.8. It does not generate historical functional failures. The inherited evaluator in `tools/power_sim_v35.py` computes historical working totals as `S(~hu)`, treating every resolved historical implementation as working. Yet sections 5.5 and 6.2 explicitly allow historical functional failure and author-dependent working composition. The frozen-frame rerun changes counts and construction settings but does not explicitly expand this working-status model.

   **Required change:** Extend the mandatory prelaunch simulation validation to include historical functional failures and author-specific working probabilities correlated with protocol difficulty and vulnerability, recalibrating exact conditional contrasts and applying the existing error, coverage, and power gates to those scenarios.

3. **The confirmation cap can conceal a consequential difference in detection difficulty between authors.**

   **Problem:** A common time allowance provides procedural symmetry, but it can preferentially count simple vulnerabilities. This matters particularly when historical defects require complicated economic reasoning while generated defects admit short demonstrations, or vice versa.

   **Evidence:** Sections 5.7 and 6.2 classify candidates cut by the confirmation cap as “not confirmed,” reserving unresolved treatment for a secondary bound. The allowance includes correcting and packaging up to three candidates within 10.68 minutes. Section 5.9’s seeded validation measures selected injected patterns, while the natural investigation samples broad campaign categories rather than explicitly guaranteeing coverage of cap-cut cases. The proposal acknowledges unequal sensitivity but does not establish how much this particular operational choice changes the result.

   **Required change:** Within the existing natural-investigation budget, preregister a probability sample specifically of cap-cut campaigns across authors and strata, with extended independent confirmation and a weighted estimate of the resulting change in the principal contrast.

4. **The reduced power target is justified by affordability more clearly than by scientific usefulness.**

   **Problem:** The study may consume the protected year while leaving the principal question broadly unresolved. Honest acknowledgment of this possibility is necessary, but does not establish that the proposed precision is sufficient for a top-venue empirical contribution.

   **Evidence:** Section 6.3 reports lowest +0.10 power of 0.395 even without unresolved outcomes in V33, very low power for the tested negative differences, and a requested minimum of 0.60 only at +0.15. RQ1a is now descriptive. Closely related work already covers Solidity function generation ([SolEval](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.218/)), historical vulnerability reconstruction ([SecureVibeBench](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1107/)), and human–agent comparisons ([Sajadi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02976/)); all are marked VERIFIED in the supplied literature review. The distinctive empirical insight therefore depends on obtaining an informative comparison, not merely completing the combined procedure.

   **Required change:** Add a preregistered scientific-adequacy criterion specifying the interval precision needed to answer the substantive comparison, justified independently of the currently affordable allocation and evaluated in the final-frame simulations.

## Questions for authors

**OQ1 — Student commitment and timed case.** Apart from item 10, I consider the proposal **borderline**, not yet a weak accept, because weaknesses 2–4 remain. If the dated commitment and timed case were supplied and demonstrated that the prescribed caps and roles work, my score would rise from **2 to 3**. Completing weakness 1 is therefore a concrete score-raising change.

**OQ2.1 — Make RQ1b principal: approve.** It directly addresses the motivating comparison. Retaining RQ1a as principal despite inadequate precision would not improve the research. Its descriptive status must remain explicit.

**OQ2.2 — Reduce minimum power to 0.60 at +0.15: approve with a condition.** Lower power is not inherently unsound, but approval depends on the scientific-adequacy criterion in weakness 4. The achievable effect size alone cannot define what makes the study useful.

**OQ2.3 — Replace unresolved-rate certification with assumptions and a diagnostic: approve with a condition.** Retain adverse bounds and unconditional reporting of the fixed sample, and complete the expanded validation in weakness 2. I do not regard a non-firing diagnostic as readiness evidence: V38 reports only 0.053 main-study firing probability under protocol-wide failures at historical unresolved rate 0.065.

**OQ2.4 — Replace the construction-yield confidence gate: approve with a condition.** Three development protocols cannot credibly certify high stratum-specific yields. Approval depends on retaining the promised final-frame validation using observed development yields, including a setting where V construction fails despite adequate pooled yield. The pooled warning alone cannot protect a two-stratum estimand.
