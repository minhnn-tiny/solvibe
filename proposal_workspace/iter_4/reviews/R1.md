# Review R1 (SE PC member: ICSE, FSE, ASE), iteration 4

Proposal reviewed: `proposal_workspace/iter_4/proposal.md` (version 25).

## Summary

The proposal plans one protected study. It replaces one function body at a time in audited Solidity snapshots. Claude Code and Codex CLI each write two attempts per function, and the historical developer body is the baseline. Twenty-two protocol audits are drawn with probability proportional to their count of ordinary functions. Each audit gives up to one function linked to a High or Medium finding (stratum V) and six other functions (stratum N).

Each implementation gets its own audit-blind campaign of Foundry fuzzing and two LLM reviewers. A flaw counts only when three things hold:
- a PoV shows it;
- a correction to that body alone removes it;
- two adjudicators accept it.

The authors now ask for four changes to earlier committee decisions:
- RQ1b becomes the principal question. It compares detected vulnerability among working implementations, agents minus historical code, weighted by the shares of the constructible strata.
- The flagged-only comparison (RQ1a) becomes descriptive.
- The power requirement drops to 0.60 at an exact +0.15 difference, inside a stated region of unresolved-outcome rates.
- Development no longer certifies construction yields or unresolved rates. A fixed diagnostic and a construction warning take their place.

Besides the proposal, my previous review and the rebuttal (`iter_3/rebuttal.md`), I checked these files:
- The strict recount of the 25-audit sample: its README and the per-audit table (`evidence/frame_audit/strict/per_audit_strict.csv`). I recomputed the totals 54, 109 and 3,620, the medians 1, 4 and 92, and the counts of audits with none: 10, 1 and 0.
- The frame audit README (`evidence/frame_audit/README.md`), which defines the 70-audit list.
- The V33, V37 and V38 results files, and the V22, V29, V30 and V37 scripts. The V37 power table, the error bound 0.0408, the coverage bound 0.9567 and the V38 firing rates in the proposal all match the results files.
- The blind rediscovery results (`evidence/rediscovery/RESULTS.md`) and the automated timing record (`evidence/timings.md`).
- The development kit (`dev_case/README.md`, `dev_case/commitment.md`, `dev_case/timesheet.csv`).

I also ran a short resampling in memory, without writing any file. It applies the proposal's rule for inclusion probabilities to the strict N counts. I use it in Weakness 2.

**Status of my iteration-3 weaknesses**

1. Allocation does not fit the 45-hour cap: **partly resolved.** The caps now add up to 40 hours per protocol and 880 hours in all, inside the 900 available. But the fit comes from caps set at the middle planning rates, not from measured costs. No rule picks an allocation from month-1 costs. The risk has moved into construction and confirmation (Weaknesses 1 and 5).
2. Count V functions in the refreshed frame and rerun the simulations: **resolved.** The strict recount gives 54 V functions from observed fixes, with 10 of 25 audits having none, and 109 when recommendations are included. V33 to V38 use both lists.
3. Functional checks that endorse the flaw: **resolved.** Section 5.5 adopts the rule I asked for. Author-blind adjudicators decide, the check is removed for every implementation, and L is recomputed before locking.
4. "Little difference" cannot be concluded: **resolved.** That reading is gone, and RQ1a is descriptive.
5. Recall gap not covered for the design difference: **resolved.** Section 6.5 now gives the latent design difference with separate recall by author and stratum, over the full feasible ranges.
6. No RQ1b precision target: **resolved in form only.** There is now a target: power 0.60 at +0.15. But the proposal does not argue that +0.15 is the smallest difference that matters (Weakness 3).

## Strengths

1. **RQ1b as principal asks a question existing benchmarks do not answer.** In the strict recount, V is 54 of 3,674 eligible functions (1.5%), or 109 of 3,729 (2.9%) with recommendations. So RQ1b is almost entirely a comparison on ordinary functions that the developer wrote and the audit did not flag. SecureVibeBench and similar benchmarks sample only tasks where the human failed. The literature review finds no verified paper with such a control group (`lit_review.md`, gap G1).
2. **The frame now rests on counted data, not a model.** The strict recount separates 94 colliding names and applies the body-only rule to every observed fix. It states its own limits: 134 rows without a second reader, 18 root causes outside functions, and agent readers rather than people. Every number I recomputed matches.
3. **The inference is checked on a wider domain than the power claim.** V37 evaluates all 76 ordered rate vectors under four mechanisms for unresolved outcomes. Error and coverage must pass on all of them, while power must pass only inside the retained region. The final check on the frozen frame keeps that scope, and its Bonferroni counts are recomputed rather than copied.
4. **The rules for closing outcomes treat both authors the same way.** Section 6.2 separates completed disagreement ("not confirmed") from inability to judge or running out of time ("unresolved"). It applies one rule per source to historical code and to both agents.
5. **The development kit makes the plan auditable.** `dev_case/README.md` turns sections 5 and 8 into timed steps, with the cap for each step, a timesheet format and the launch decision sequence. A PC member can see exactly what the student will do and when the study stops.

## Weaknesses

1. **The confirmation and review caps are set per implementation, at the average workload. By the proposal's own planning rates, no single candidate can be confirmed or adjudicated within them.**
   - **Problem: confirmation.**
     - Each implementation gets 0.178 student hours, about 11 minutes, "shared by confirmation, correction, packaging and primary lock administration" (5.7). No time moves between implementations.
     - The planning rates behind that figure are 0.178 candidates per campaign and 1.0 hour per candidate, with a range of 0.5 to 1.5 hours (`tools/power_sim_v22.py`, line 21).
     - So 0.178 hours is the expected time averaged over all implementations, and most implementations have no candidate. An implementation that does have one gets 11 minutes for work the plan itself prices at 30 to 90 minutes.
     - LLM help may shorten this work. But nothing measured shows that a person can confirm, correct and package a candidate in 11 minutes.
   - **Problem: review.**
     - Each adjudicator has at most 121 hours. "Within each source, divide reserved hours by the maximum number of scheduled implementations that can produce packages" (8).
     - The main study schedules up to 22 × 7 × 5 = 770 implementations. Even if one adjudicator's whole 121 hours went to main work, each implementation would get about 0.16 hours, or 9 to 10 minutes.
     - The proposal's own workload assumption is 0.5 hours per package.
   - **Evidence of the consequences.**
     - A cut candidate is "not confirmed" (6.2). So the study can record almost no confirmed flaws while every step stays valid. The development decision does not catch this. Step 5 treats recorded outcomes as legitimate and names no share of cut candidates that counts as failure.
     - A package that reaches review and runs out of time is "unresolved". Suppose packages reach review at anything like the planning candidate rate (8/64 to 3/13 per implementation), and each needs 0.5 hours. Then the unresolved share is about 0.12 to 0.23, against the region's limit of 0.013 for agents. The study would leave the power region by design, not by chance.
     - The cap may also favour one author. A flaw that is quick to show, such as a missing check, can be confirmed in 11 minutes. Finding 63008 is a boundary error in a tick iterator that lets an attacker take another provider's rewards. Building its package took an LLM agent 23 minutes, with the flaw already known and pilot material prepared (`evidence/timings.md`). If agent flaws on ordinary functions are more often the quick kind, the cap creates part of a positive difference. The seeded pairs cannot show this, because they put the same pattern into both authors' code.
   - **Required change:** Set the student's confirmation allowance per candidate, at the 1.0-hour planning rate, and each adjudicator's allowance per package, at the 0.5-hour assumption. Fund the totals at a high quantile of the simulated candidate count. If a reserve still runs out, preregister random subsampling of the remaining candidates, with recorded selection probabilities that enter the weights. In expectation this costs the same student time as now (770 × 0.178 ≈ 137 hours).

2. **The simulations use frames of 200 protocols, but the only frame the proposal has listed has 70 audits. Sampling a third to a half of a small frame has not been tested.**
   - **Problem.**
     - V33 to V38 use "24 fixed simulated frames ... Each has 200 protocols" (6.3; `M = 200` in `tools/power_sim_v29.py`).
     - The frame audit takes the Code4rena, Sherlock and Cantina audits from 2025 or later that have a downloaded finding, and removes pilot protocols. "70 audits remain" (`evidence/frame_audit/README.md`).
     - Eight of the first 33 audits in the random order were replaced because the code or scope was unavailable or the code was not Solidity. At that rate, about 53 of the 70 are usable.
     - The proposal does not state the planned size of the frozen frame.
   - **Evidence.**
     - I resampled the 25 strict N counts in memory and applied the proposal's rule for inclusion probabilities with 22 protocols.
     - With 200 protocols, no protocol is selected with certainty. With 67 protocols, about 3 of the 22 are. With 50 protocols, about 6 are. The sampling fraction rises from 0.11 to 0.33 or 0.44.
     - The delete-one-protocol jackknife treats every sampled protocol as a random draw. Nothing in sections 6.3 or 8 says how protocols selected with certainty, or a large sampling fraction, enter it.
     - The interval may be wider than needed, and power may fall below 0.60. The month-2 check would find this. But then "the preferred allocation does not launch and the design returns to review" in the month the study should start. Replacement calibration can only lower the multiplier to 1.0, the bottom of the V30 grid.
   - **Required change:** Before acceptance, rerun the V37 checks on frames of the size the study will actually use. For example, use 50 and 70 protocols resampled from the strict counts, less three development protocols. State in section 6.3 how protocols selected with certainty enter the jackknife.

3. **The +0.15 power target is not argued to be the smallest difference that matters. Smaller differences have little chance of a directional result, and the design cannot show that agents are safer.**
   - **Problem.**
     - The planning model sets the historical latent rate on N functions at 0.05 or 0.10, and recall at 0.5 to 0.8 (`tools/power_sim_v29.py`, `tools/power_sim_v30.py`). So the historical detected rate is about 0.025 to 0.08.
     - A +0.15 difference means that agents' working ordinary functions show detected flaws at 0.175 to 0.23. That is three to seven times the historical rate, and it needs a latent agent rate of about 0.22 to 0.46.
     - The proposal gives no evidence that an effect this large is plausible on unflagged functions. The verified figures concern tasks chosen because the human failed: SecureVibeBench reports 21.8% and 26.7% (https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22097).
   - **Evidence.**
     - At +0.10 with no unresolved outcomes, V33 gives power 0.395 in the worst frame and 0.58 at the median. At unresolved rate 0.013, the worst frame gives 0.267 (`tools/power_results_v33.txt`). Power at +0.05 is not reported.
     - Negative targets reach only −0.011 to −0.045. Power there is 0.005 in the worst frame and about 0.05 at the median. So a finding that agents are safer cannot come out.
     - The expected half-width of the RQ1b interval is reported nowhere. V37 reports half-widths only for RQ1a.
     - The most likely result of the ICSE paper is therefore "inconclusive". Such a paper can still be accepted if its interval rules out differences that practitioners care about. At present nobody can tell whether it will.
   - **Required change:** Add a table to section 6.3, made with the V37 generator. For each recall pattern and historical N rate, it gives the mean RQ1b half-width and the correct-direction power at +0.05 and +0.10. Then state in section 3 the smallest agent excess that a non-directional result would rule out.

4. **Comparing only working outputs can hide an agent excess, and neither the analysis plan nor the simulations test this.**
   - **Problem.**
     - RQ1b compares rates among working implementations. Suppose agents fail more often on complex functions, and complex functions are more often vulnerable. Then the agents' working set is easier than the historical set, and their rate is lower for reasons that have nothing to do with security.
     - The proposal says this itself: "Working outputs can have different function mixtures across authors" (6.2).
     - Shaw shows that code size alone can create or hide differences between human and AI code (VERIFIED in the literature review; https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28021).
   - **Evidence.**
     - The simulator makes agent working status independent of the function: `work = (rng.random(...) < 0.9) & (rng.random(...) < 0.8)` in `tools/power_sim_v37.py`. Historical code always works.
     - So V37 never tests a link between failing to work and being vulnerable.
     - The D decomposition shows how much functional failure contributes. It does not compare the two authors on the same functions.
   - **Required change:** Preregister a secondary comparison restricted to functions where the historical body works and at least one attempt of the configuration works. Compute both rates on that same set of functions. Report how many functions it keeps, by stratum and configuration.

5. **One 2.25-hour construction cap for every function may leave stratum V nearly empty, and the warning rule cannot see it.**
   - **Problem.**
     - Every sampled function gets 2.25 hours (8). The reason is sound: different caps could reveal the stratum label.
     - But version 15 of this proposal priced V construction at 4 to 8 hours and N at 1.5 to 3 hours (my iteration-3 review, Weakness 1). The current cost model uses one build rate of 1.5 to 3 hours for all functions (`tools/power_sim_v22.py`).
     - If the earlier V estimate was right, most V tasks will fail construction. RQ1b barely notices, because V carries 1.5% to 2.9% of the weight. But RQ1a, audit-assisted rediscovery (5.8) and one of the four sources of detection evidence all need constructed V functions.
   - **Evidence.**
     - The construction warning pools V and N. The proposal says it has not been shown to detect "poor V construction hidden by N outcomes" (6.8).
     - The simulations draw V and N construction from the same distribution, with one yield threshold (`Frame` in `tools/power_sim_v29.py`). So they never try a low V yield.
   - **Required change:** Add a stratum V rule to step 7 of the development procedure. If none of the V functions sampled across the three cases is built within the cap, the design returns to review. Report V construction time against the cap in the timed case.

6. **The student's dated commitment and the timed case are still empty.**
   - **Problem.** The iteration-3 committee made both a precondition for acceptance. Without a single human-timed step, every cap in section 8 is still a planning number.
   - **Evidence.** The commitment form (`dev_case/commitment.md`) has no entries. The timesheet (`dev_case/timesheet.csv`) holds only its header row. Section 8 says so plainly.
   - **Required change:** Supply the filled commitment and the timesheet for case 1. Case 1 must take at least one candidate through confirmation and both adjudications within the stated caps. If no natural candidate arises, use a seeded flaw from the pattern catalogue that is outside the discovery-gate reserve.

## Ratings

- C1: pass — No verified paper compares agent and historical code on a probability sample that includes unflagged functions, with executable, body-attributed labels, and the proposal presents this as an empirical contribution rather than a new method.
- C2: pass — Survey and loss evidence show that the problem is real, and the comparison on ordinary functions is the one developers would act on.
- C3: pass — Blind campaigns for each implementation, body-only attribution, construction decided before outcomes and weighted estimation with adverse bounds go well beyond applying an LLM to a task.
- C4: concern — Caps set per implementation can turn detection into a test of how fast a flaw can be shown, possibly favouring one author (Weakness 1), and comparing only working outputs can hide an agent excess (Weakness 4).
- C5: pass — Attacker capabilities, capital, transaction ordering, oracle behaviour and severity rules are explicit and recorded for each protocol.
- C6: concern — Baselines, metrics, validation and threats are named, but the RQ1b half-width is not reported, the +0.15 target is not justified, and the simulated frames are three to four times larger than the only listed frame (Weaknesses 2 and 3).
- C7: concern — The totals now fit (880 of 900 main hours, about 120 of 150 development hours), which lifts my earlier fail, but the fit rests on per-implementation caps that no person has tested, and the student's commitment is still empty (Weaknesses 1 and 6).
- C8: pass — Offline execution, disclosure with a 90-day re-check, an embargo while deployments are exposed, local seeds and an institutional check are all in place.

## Questions for authors

1. In development step 5, what share of candidates cut by the confirmation cap would you count as "required work cannot be performed validly"? If the answer is none, how would development detect a cap that is too small?
2. What time window and which platforms will the frozen frame cover, and how many audits do you expect it to hold?
3. Will the paper report the share of audited High and Medium findings that the one-body rule excludes, by severity? Samples of linked bug fixes are known to lean toward less severe bugs (Bird et al., ESEC/FSE 2009, https://www.cs.ucdavis.edu/~filkov/papers/biasbusters.pdf). Readers will ask whether the same holds for one-body fixes.
4. Under protocol-wide failures, the development diagnostic fires in at most 0.012 of samples, even at a historical unresolved rate of 0.065 (`tools/power_results_v38.txt`). It cannot change the analysis. As a launch gate, what does it add beyond reporting unresolved counts by author and stratum?
5. Compute for the native agents and the two LLM reviewers is still unpriced. What is the estimate per implementation, and who funds it?

## Open questions

**OQ1. Apart from item 10, does the proposal meet the bar for my score? If item 10 were supplied and showed the caps to be workable, what score would I give?**

Apart from item 10, no. Weakness 1 is a design problem that does not depend on the student. Weakness 2 can be checked now by computation alone.

Item 10 can answer Weakness 1 with evidence, but only if the timed case exercises confirmation and review:
- Suppose case 1 takes at least one candidate through confirmation within 0.178 hours, and through both adjudications within the review time each implementation gets. Then the caps are shown to be workable. With the rerun in Weakness 2, I would give 4.
- Suppose instead the case produces no candidate. Then it shows that construction and campaign operation fit, but says nothing about confirmation or review. My score would stay at 3 until the change in Weakness 1 is made.

**OQ2. The four requested changes.**

1. **RQ1b as principal, RQ1a secondary: approve, with a condition.** Last round I asked for an RQ1b target because it is the question practitioners ask. RQ1a cannot reach useful precision at any allocation examined: the V37 half-width is up to 0.711. Condition: preregister the comparison on the same functions described in Weakness 4, and report every headline RQ1b result beside the D difference.
2. **Power 0.60 at +0.15: approve, with a condition.** A first study with costly executable labels can accept less than 0.80, if it is framed as estimation and readers know its precision. Condition: add the half-width table and the stated smallest excess ruled out (Weakness 3). Also add a sentence in section 3 saying that, at this size, the study cannot conclude that agents are safer.
3. **Conditional readiness for unresolved outcomes: approve, with a condition.** Adverse bounds stay in the principal analysis, so a departure from the operating region costs power, not validity. That is the right trade. Condition: fix the caps first (Weakness 1). As written, the review time each implementation gets would make unresolved outcomes common by design, and no diagnostic should be asked to catch that.
4. **Descriptive construction yields with a pooled warning: approve, with a condition.** Three cases cannot certify a yield, so the old gate could never be met. Condition: add the stratum V rule from Weakness 5, because the pooled warning cannot see V construction fail.

## Overall score

Score: 3

The revision does what I asked in iteration 3 on the frame count, the checks that endorse flaws, the "little difference" reading and recall sensitivity. Making RQ1b principal is the right choice for an SE venue.

But the budget now fits because each cap is set at the average workload per implementation. By the proposal's own rates, a single candidate cannot be confirmed or reviewed within those caps. The study could therefore launch and record almost no confirmed flaws. The simulations also assume a frame three to four times larger than the only one the proposal has listed.

Three changes would raise my score to 4:
- per-candidate and per-package allowances, with a preregistered subsampling rule (Weakness 1);
- the V37 rerun on frames of realistic size, with a stated rule for protocols selected with certainty (Weakness 2);
- the filled commitment and a timed case that exercises confirmation and review (Weakness 6).

Confidence: 4
