# Rebuttal to the iteration 1 meta-review

Authors: Peer A (Claude Code) and Peer B (Codex). Peer A wrote the first draft. Peer B amended 14 of its decisions; where an amendment changed the decision, the row says "[Peer B amendment]". Both peers then revised the proposal over Loop 1 rounds 6 to 10. Peer B checked this rebuttal against the final proposal and the result files, and its corrections are applied. This rebuttal answers every required change in `iter_1/meta_review.md` and points to the revised proposal, `proposal_v11.md`.

**Stagnation rule.** Two Blocking issues (feasibility, item 1; equal search for flaws, item 2) were raised in Loop 1 before iteration 1 and fixed there, and the committee raised them again. The new fixes are structurally different, not refinements:

- Feasibility. The earlier fix kept the full design and added a month-two gate. The new fix replaces the design with a small core study (paper 1), moves everything else to named extensions or a second paper, and gives a costed plan with stated hour assumptions, totals and a reserve.
- Equal search. The earlier fix matched discovery budgets and replayed PoVs across authors. The new fix changes the primary label: an implementation counts as vulnerable only if its own audit-blind campaign finds a flaw that is then confirmed. Pooled replay and audit-derived attacks become secondary views. Planned validation includes matched seeded flaws, natural audit rediscovery, and independent investigation. These provide separate evidence about detection limits; they do not establish equal recall for natural flaws.

**Evidence produced since iteration 1.** Simulation results below come from the saved code and outputs under `proposal_workspace/tools/`. Pilot observations come from the repository's pilot reports. Allocations and person-hour budgets are planning choices and assumptions, not measured study results. Development measurements must replace the cost assumptions before main collection. The simulation files are:

- `power_sim_v10.py` (results `power_results_v10_a.txt`, `_b.txt`, `_c.txt`): RQ1 inference calibrated on one random stream and validated on an independent one, including equivalence checked on data generated at the true ±0.05 boundary as a family decision; paper 2's power with one common outcome; seeded-validation precision.
- `power_sim_v11.py` (results `power_results_v11.txt`, `_calib.txt`, `_v4.txt`): precision of the comparison between the V-only estimate and the weighted estimate.
- Earlier files (`power_results_v8_core.txt`, `power_results_v9*.txt`) are superseded; `power_results_v9.txt` carries a provenance note.

## Disagreements among reviewers

1. Which way detection differences bias the result: no net direction is assumed. Own-campaign labels remove the extra primary discovery opportunities caused by pooled replay. Four views separate own discovery, pooled discovery, audit supplementation, and their union. Seeded validation, audit rediscovery, and independent investigation provide separate evidence. They do not establish natural-flaw recall. Sensitivity analysis reports assumptions under which the latent difference reverses sign; if plausible ranges reverse it, prevalence is reported as not identified (item 2).
2. Which endpoint is primary: D from the implementation's own campaign (item 5).
3. AI-assisted human functions: kept in the primary population, with the unflagged subset as a secondary analysis. This follows R1 and R3, not R5 or the AC's threshold, for the reason in item 3.
4. Shrink or add: protect the smaller core. The minimum planning allocation is 15 protocols. Expansion to 20 protocols or up to four V functions requires the stated cost and inference checks. Power is reported for the simulated settings, with explicit limits on smaller effects and interpretation of null results. Other experiments have separate allocations and cannot consume paper 1's protected resources (items 1, 6, and 7).
5. Security-specific RQ2: R4's matched control is the confirmatory test; R2's trajectory analysis is secondary (item 8).
6. Natural fault-enforcing tests: begin screening in month one. Use a census only if measured costs permit; otherwise estimate prevalence from sampled V functions with semantic confirmation, weighted uncertainty, and unresolved-case bounds (item 9).
7. Ratings: novelty rests on items 7 and 21; soundness on items 1 to 3.
