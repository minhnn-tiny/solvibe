## Summary

The proposal studies function-body replacements by two coding agents in audited Solidity repositories. Its main contribution is comparing an audit-flagged-only estimate with a probability-weighted estimate that also includes unflagged functions, using independently discovered and executable vulnerabilities. A conditional second study examines whether executing faulty security assertions undermines PoV-guided repair beyond the effect of ordinary incorrect assertions.

The proposal is unusually explicit about what its measurements cannot establish. Nevertheless, the protected study is not yet convincingly sized for its distinguishing research question, and critical parts of the discovery and human-work pipeline remain unvalidated. I regard it as promising but borderline.

## Strengths

- The historical-developer baseline avoids an unjustified human-only provenance claim. The proposal also correctly distinguishes replacement in mature repositories from a causal comparison of human and agent productivity.
- The threat model, entitlement-based severity rubric, and function-only correction requirement provide substantial technical substance beyond running agents and counting static warnings.
- Independent primary discovery, subsequent pooled replay, and audit-assisted replay are separated clearly. This makes otherwise hidden advantages from audit knowledge and repeated agent attempts observable.
- Compilation failures, functional failures, unresolved evaluations, and missed vulnerabilities receive explicit treatment. The adverse-bound analysis is particularly valuable.
- The proposal acknowledges weak power, imperfect calibration, preliminary pilot evidence, and the conditional status of paper 2. Offline execution and disclosure controls are appropriate.

## Weaknesses

1. **The distinctive contribution is secondary and only precise for very large effects.**  
   **Evidence:** Section 3 identifies the sampling-design comparison as paper 1’s contribution, while §6.3 makes that comparison secondary unless separately preregistered. Section 6.4 reports calibrated mean half-widths of 0.252 and 0.211 and poor detection of moderate design differences. Function regeneration already appears in [SolEval](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.218/), and same-task developer–agent comparisons appear in [Sajadi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02976). Consequently, an inconclusive design comparison could leave mostly an expensive application of established ideas. A small effect would be informative only if estimated tightly enough to distinguish it from a consequential one.  
   **Required change:** Make the V-only-minus-population contrast a primary endpoint with a prespecified practically meaningful precision target, and choose the core allocation against that target under the measured budget. Satisfying this requirement would materially raise my score.

2. **Audit blindness is specified for construction and automated discovery, but not sufficiently for human confirmation.**  
   **Evidence:** Section 5.4 explicitly has the student open audits after freezing blind artifacts and construct audit-informed PoVs and corrections. Sections 5.7 and 8 then require human candidate confirmation, while independent adjudicators assess the resulting packages. The proposal does not clearly identify whether an audit-exposed person may develop a primary candidate into an executable attack or correction. Such assistance could disproportionately rescue historical V candidates even when their initial discovery was blind; blinded final adjudication does not eliminate that upstream asymmetry.  
   **Required change:** Specify and enforce a primary-confirmation workflow in which attack completion and correction construction receive only the originating campaign materials and permitted context, with audit-derived assistance recorded exclusively in the secondary analysis.

3. **The natural-implementation validation has no defined evidential resolution.**  
   **Evidence:** Section 5.9 promises independent investigation across authors, strata, and positive, negative, and unresolved results, but §8 leaves the number \(E\) unspecified and provides only “about 100 auditor hours.” Meanwhile, the 60 seeded pairs cannot establish comparable recall, and seeded patterns do not identify natural-flaw sensitivity (§6.5). The proposal therefore lacks a concrete account of what its most relevant independent validation can discover or exclude. This matters even for interpreting detected-outcome differences as practically useful security evidence.  
   **Required change:** Add a prespecified natural-validation sampling plan that converts the auditor-hour allowance into implementation counts, allocation probabilities, investigation budgets, and anticipated uncertainty for campaign–auditor disagreement by author.

4. **The twelve-month feasibility claim depends on an unmeasured end-to-end process.**  
   **Evidence:** Section 8 acknowledges that prior human-review roles were performed by LLM subagents. At the upper planning rates, the 15-protocol core leaves 357 student hours for development, failed construction, campaign setup and supervision, replay, and infrastructure repair. The 63008 example still lacks its complete entitlement and function-only attribution package (§5.11). The proposed launch gates are sensible, but they are future safeguards rather than evidence that a first-year student can deliver this workload.  
   **Required change:** Supply the proposed timed development report covering three complete cases, including probability-selected N functions and actual independent human reviews, and use its measured costs to demonstrate one affordable core allocation.

5. **Paper 2 does not operationalize the matching needed for its security-specific interpretation.**  
   **Evidence:** Section 6.6 defines a useful common vulnerability outcome and a three-way contrast, but “matched non-security behavior” remains unspecified. A wrong assertion directly endorsing the vulnerability and an unrelated wrong output assertion can differ in repair locality, conflict with the shown PoV, difficulty, and relevance to the target body. A positive contrast could reflect these differences rather than security-specific obedience. This distinction is central given prior manipulation of conflicting tests and feedback in [ImpossibleBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20270).  
   **Required change:** Define a matched-control construction and validation protocol that balances assertion salience, repair effort, target-code overlap, and conflict with supplied evidence before admitting a task to paper 2.

## Ratings

C1: concern — The sampling comparison is a defensible empirical distinction, but its expected resolution and the second study’s distinguishing mechanism remain uncertain.

C2: pass — Developer adoption and consequential smart-contract failures establish a meaningful problem without requiring unsupported claims about AI-caused incidents.

C3: pass — Executable attribution, independent discovery, sampling inference, and controlled repair experiments provide substantial methodological depth.

C4: concern — Claims are carefully bounded, but the human-confirmation information boundary and natural-validation design remain incomplete.

C5: pass — Attacker capabilities, environmental assumptions, asset entitlements, severity, and excluded behaviors are explicit.

C6: concern — The evaluation is extensive, but the main novelty-bearing contrast has weak precision and natural validation lacks a concrete allocation.

C7: concern — The protected scope is plausible only conditionally; no complete human-reviewed throughput measurement yet supports the annual budget.

C8: pass — Offline experiments, exposure checks, embargoes, paid reviewers, and institutional-review checks address the principal ethical issues.

## Questions for authors

- Can an audit-exposed constructor assist a primary candidate after its initial discovery, and exactly which assistance would disqualify it from the primary endpoint?
- What precision would make a near-zero sampling-design difference scientifically useful?
- How many natural implementations can two independent auditors investigate within the stated allowance?
- What concrete paired assertions demonstrate that paper 2’s ordinary-error control has comparable difficulty and conflict with the supplied evidence?

## Overall score

Score: 3

The project has a credible research direction and strong attention to validity. A primary allocation justified by useful precision, together with measured end-to-end feasibility, would move it toward acceptance.
