## Strengths

- **The contribution is now clearly separated from established techniques.** The proposal measures a selection effect rather than claiming that weighting, executable checking or historical comparison is new.
- **The population restriction is explicit.** Construction failure is distinguished from failure to supply the promised procedure, and estimated constructible stratum shares enter each uncertainty replicate.
- **The attribution example has materially improved.** The one-body patch preserves empty-range behavior; fixed-state assertions check entitlement, backing and withdrawal; stored results cover the independent H-02 correction. This supports the executable example without establishing human adjudication or audit blindness.
- **The repair objection was addressed substantively.** Matched endorsed outcomes and contrary evidence remove the specific outcome mismatch identified previously.
- **The proposal does not disguise failed readiness as success.** It explicitly withholds launch and rejects an unspecified benchmark-paper fallback.

## Weaknesses

1. **There is still no demonstrated information supply for the distinctive contribution.**

   **Evidence:** Section 6.4 requires a maximum scenario-specific mean full-interval half-width of 0.20 and at least 0.80 probability of excluding −0.10 near a true design difference of −0.33.

   The saved V19 results meet those criteria under the earlier V-count assumption without unresolved outcomes: half-width **0.167**, exclusion probability **0.83**. Under the counted pilot distribution, the corresponding figures are **0.314** and **0.38**. The fix-scope record identifies 17 one-body findings, nine from one protocol, with three protocols contributing none.

   V20 incorporates construction losses, but its source generates construction independently of outcomes and uses model-based domain truth. With construction probabilities V=0.7 and N=0.9, even the no-unresolved scenario gives exclusion probability **0.71**. The proposal correctly acknowledges that neither this simulation nor the filtered pilot frame establishes final readiness.

   This is not a demand for a guaranteed significant result. It is a request to establish that the primary question can receive an informative answer under a plausible available frame.

   **Required change:** Produce one frame-based readiness analysis using the refreshed eligible counts and a fully enumerated simulated finite population, including difficulty-dependent construction and unresolved outcomes, demonstrating that the fixed allocation meets the declared RQ1a criteria with Monte Carlo uncertainty accounted for.

2. **The fixed ceilings constrain expenditure but do not yet establish a feasible twelve-month study.**

   **Evidence:** Section 8 reserves **45 student hours per protocol**, while its own assumed average workload ranges from approximately **43 to 100 hours**. The maximum-slot reservation must also fit, not merely the average. Construction yield affects the primary domain and precision, so reducing successful construction cannot independently solve the workload problem.

   `evidence/timings.md` explicitly records automated, LLM-assisted work starting from existing pilot material. It does not measure the human roles whose availability and cost govern this design. The student’s participation remains unconfirmed, compute is unpriced, and no complete human development case exists.

   The new stop rule prevents an uncontrolled study; it does not show that a first-year student can deliver the intended contribution. This concern is stronger because the minimum allocation has increased and the proposal provides no accepted scientific substitute if development fails.

   **Required change:** Complete the specified three human development cases, including probability-selected N functions and all failed attempts, and use that record to reconcile the fixed construction reservations, staffing and funded resource ceilings.
