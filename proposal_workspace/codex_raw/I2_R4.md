## Weaknesses

1. **The distinctive contribution is now measured, but the protected allocation may estimate it too imprecisely to support an informative paper.**

   **Evidence:** Section 7 makes the sampling-design comparison the first contribution, yet Section 6.3 leaves that comparison secondary unless separately added to the multiplicity plan. Section 6.4 and the saved `tools/power_results_v11_calib.txt` agree on a mean calibrated half-width of **0.252** at 15 protocols. The recorded power for design differences around 0.18–0.26 is only **0.25–0.48**. Thus a substantial selection effect can remain unresolved. The proposal correctly acknowledges this; acknowledgement does not establish that the minimum deliverable answers its distinctive question.

   The prior-work table above matters here: another historical-code comparison with stronger checking has a narrower novelty margin than a useful quantitative account of selection effects. A ranking reversal is unnecessary, but an interval compatible with materially different conclusions is not automatically informative.

   **Required change:** Make the sampling-design comparison the basis of a preregistered precision-based allocation rule, specifying a scientifically useful maximum interval width and selecting a funded allocation that meets it under development-informed scenarios.

2. **The ordinary-error control does not yet exclude generic test obedience as the explanation for a positive “security-specific” contrast.**

   **Evidence:** Section 6.6 uses vulnerability retention in every arm and requires positive \(\theta=I_{\mathrm{security}}-I_{\mathrm{ordinary}}\). However, an agent that indiscriminately follows incorrect assertions can produce this result: the security assertion directly requires the measured vulnerability, while an ordinary incorrect assertion requires a different error. The latter can be obeyed without retaining the vulnerability. The experiment identifies differential effects on vulnerability retention, but that alone does not show a mechanism beyond generic obedience.

   Relevant established overlaps are:

   - [*ImpossibleBench: Measuring LLMs’ Propensity of Exploiting Test Cases* — ICLR 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.20270v1): manipulates specification–test conflicts, test access and feedback; the overlapping technique is controlled misleading-test exposure.
   - [*On the Risks of using LLM-Generated Tests for Regression Testing* — arXiv preprint, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11835): establishes tests that encode and preserve faulty behavior; the overlapping construct is fault enforcement.
   - [*SpecGuard: Proving a Task Is Broken Before the Agent Cheats* — arXiv preprint, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09159v1): Figure 2 already illustrates removal of a session-fixation defense to satisfy a corrupted test. Consequently, security harm from conflicting tests is also established, although its formal conflict-detection technique differs from this proposal.

   None establishes the proposed matched PoV experiment. Nevertheless, the control must do more than show that assertions targeting a vulnerability affect that vulnerability more than unrelated assertions.

   **Required change:** Add a preregistered matched-defect-retention diagnostic that measures obedience to the endorsed error in both assertion types, with comparable explicit counterevidence, while retaining vulnerability retention as the security endpoint.

3. **The feasibility evidence still does not exercise the work that makes paper 1 distinctive.**

   **Evidence:** Section 8 explicitly describes person-hours as assumptions and schedules, rather than reports, the required development measurements. The 15-protocol plan entails up to 150 functions, 900 generated implementations and 1,050 natural discovery campaigns, before seeded validation. Its pessimistic student-work subtotal leaves 357 hours for development and operations.

   The cited pilot report describes audit-derived PoVs and acknowledges that its requests transmitted either the vulnerable design or the correction. It therefore does not demonstrate audit-blind property construction for probability-selected unflagged functions. Section 5.11 also states that the worked example’s stronger attribution package remains incomplete. These limitations are candidly disclosed, but the critical throughput remains unmeasured.

   **Required change:** Complete the promised timed, end-to-end development pilot—including probability-selected N functions, retained failures and actual independent human adjudication—and replace the minimum-deliverable budget with its measured throughput.
