## Summary

The revision makes substantial methodological progress. Paper 1 now estimates vulnerabilities detected by independent, audit-blind campaigns, separates pooled and audit-assisted replay, and protects a smaller twelve-month scope. This is a credible empirical research direction, but its executable security standard and human workload remain insufficiently demonstrated.

Disposition of my previous weaknesses:

- **Previous W1 — Partly resolved:** Sections 5.7–5.9 and 6.5 remove the primary audit-information asymmetry and specify sensitivity contours; independent investigation of natural flaws remains under-specified.
- **Previous W2 — Unresolved:** Section 5.11 explicitly acknowledges that the compliant 63008 evidence package remains incomplete; the supplied artifacts confirm the outstanding attribution and interacting-bug questions.
- **Previous W3 — Resolved at proposal level:** Section 4 replaces platform-dependent labels with a common severity framework and independent adjudication, with operational details frozen before evaluation.
- **Previous W4 — Resolved at proposal level:** Section 6.6 specifies a common vulnerability-retention outcome, a matched non-security control, and a security-specific interaction contrast with trajectory evidence.
- **Previous W5 — Partly resolved:** The protected core is substantially smaller and explicitly budgeted, but its labor assumptions have not been replaced by measurements.

My remaining concerns concern validation of the proposed study, rather than a demand that a proposal already contain its final results.

## Strengths

- The threat model specifies attacker privileges, reachable states, capital, borrowing, ordering, and environmental assumptions. Availability findings require an attacker-induced failure of legitimate operation.
- Function-body-only corrections, preserved functionality, and independent severity and attribution decisions provide a strong intended confirmation standard.
- The primary discovery design now avoids giving historical implementations audit-derived attacks while searching agent implementations without equivalent information. Secondary replay remains useful without changing the primary labels.
- The proposal explicitly distinguishes detected vulnerabilities from latent vulnerability prevalence. Its sensitivity analysis permits the conclusion that prevalence differences are unidentified.
- Unresolved outcomes receive adverse bounds, and failed generation cannot automatically support a claim of improved security.
- The revision appropriately narrows novelty relative to [SolEval](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.218/) and [ImpossibleBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20270/). Probability weighting and obedience to incorrect tests are no longer presented as inherently novel.
- Offline execution, exposure-dependent embargoes, separate treatment of seeded flaws, and disclosure restrictions that survive submission deadlines adequately address the principal dual-use risks.

## Weaknesses

1. **The natural-flaw validation is not yet an operational validation protocol.**  
   **Evidence:** Section 5.9 promises that two auditors investigate a probability sample covering authors, strata, and campaign outcomes, but section 8 leaves the sample size as \(E\), supported by approximately 100 auditor hours. No allocation or investigation budget per implementation is specified. The 60 seeded pairs cannot fill this gap: the saved `tools/power_results_v10_c.txt` supports the stated pooled half-widths of 0.135–0.194, and those concern inserted patterns, not natural flaws. The development gate tests rediscovery of historical audit vulnerabilities, not missed agent-introduced vulnerabilities in N functions. The narrower detected-outcome claim is defensible, but the proposed validation cannot yet establish how informative it will be.  
   **Required change:** Preregister a concrete natural-implementation validation protocol specifying sampling probabilities and minimum coverage across generator, audit stratum, and campaign outcome; per-implementation investigation resources; and how its results constrain the sensitivity ranges used for interpretation.

2. **No completed evidence package yet demonstrates the flagship example under the revised security standard.**  
   **Evidence:** Section 5.11 states, “That package has not been completed.” The original `pilot/tasks/63008/pov/PoV63008_StealRewards.t.sol` concludes with `assertGt(profitInEth, 0, ...)`. The stronger `loss_estimate.json` documents victim backing shortfall and failed withdrawals, but its correction replaces the entire `TickIterator.sol` file. The supplied `extension/report.md` explicitly invokes incorrectly initialized reward growth from H-02 and the ability to win a top-of-block swap when needed. These facts do not refute exploitability, but they leave the proposed attribution and attacker-resource checks unvalidated.  
   **Required change:** Complete the 63008 evidence package with executable victim-entitlement and backing assertions, a functionality-preserving target-body-only correction, an explicit capital and ordering record, and the attack outcome with H-02 corrected independently.

3. **The reduced core remains dependent on unmeasured human security work.**  
   **Evidence:** Section 8 labels its unit costs as assumptions and reserves 357 student hours for development, unsuccessful construction, campaign operation, replay, and infrastructure. Its protected allocation still entails up to 1,050 historical and agent discovery campaigns, plus seeded validation. The cited `docs/pilot/process_log.md` records subagents performing review work; the proposal correctly acknowledges that this is not measured independent-human throughput. Paid seed checks also remain to be reconciled with candidate adjudication. The reduction resolves the earlier scope inflation, but not whether the indispensable work fits.  
   **Required change:** Supply the timed end-to-end development record already specified in section 8—including probability-selected N functions and actual independent human review—and use it to replace the assumed core budget with a reconciled student-hour, paid-review, compute, and precision budget.

4. **The first paper’s stated contribution is not yet aligned with its confirmatory decision plan.**  
   **Evidence:** Sections 3 and 7 identify the comparison between flagged-only and population-weighted conclusions as the first paper’s contribution, while section 6.3 makes that comparison secondary unless separately preregistered. The saved `tools/power_results_v11_calib.txt` reports a mean calibrated half-width of 0.252 at 15 protocols. Section 6.4 acknowledges weak power for moderate design differences. Consequently, a significant author comparison could coexist with an inconclusive result on the proposed distinguishing insight; this matters particularly for a top security venue.  
   **Required change:** Make the sampling-design contrast a designated principal paper-1 analysis with a prespecified multiplicity rule and useful precision target, and base the retained allocation on that target.

## Questions for authors

- How will the natural-validation sample distribute its fixed auditor hours across historical code, both agent configurations, audit strata, and campaign outcomes?
- Which effects of 63008 survive an independent correction of H-02?
- What interval width would make the sampling-design comparison useful even if its estimate is near zero?
- Who performs candidate confirmation after audit-informed construction, and how is audit-derived information prevented from entering that confirmation process?
