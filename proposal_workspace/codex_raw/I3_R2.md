## Strengths

- **The security example now demonstrates victim harm rather than merely attacker profit.** `Fixed63008_State.t.sol` checks reward payment, backing, principal withdrawal and the attacker’s permissible reward share. The recorded corrected-state runs satisfy these checks.
- **The attribution intervention is appropriately narrow.** `tickiterator_function_body_v2.patch` adds one assignment after the empty-range return. The recorded H-02-only state remains vulnerable, while the target-body correction blocks both tested scenarios.
- **Attacker assumptions are explicit.** The proposal reports capital ceilings, the required ordering opportunity and the absence of a competitive fee-auction simulation. It does not present mock-chain amounts as deployed losses.
- **Audit blindness now extends through confirmation.** Section 5.4 prevents a builder from opening findings after request construction but before confirming primary candidates.
- **Detection limitations receive appropriately narrow interpretation.** Natural investigation, seeded detection and audit rediscovery remain separate evidence sources. Restricted natural-recall ranges require explicit transfer assumptions.
- **Construction failures cannot silently disappear.** The proposal distinguishes failure under a supplied construction procedure from failure to supply that procedure, and recomputes estimated constructible-domain shares within uncertainty replicates.
- **Ethics remain adequate.** Offline attacks, deployment-exposure checks and continued embargo protect against treating public audit status as permission to release actionable exploits.

## Weaknesses

1. **The resource ceiling has not been shown compatible with the minimum study’s required security work.**  
   **Evidence:** Section 8 allocates 45 student hours per protocol, including preparation, construction, generation, campaigns and confirmation reservations. Its own comparison gives approximately 43 hours at optimistic rates for an average allocation and approximately 100 hours at pessimistic rates; neither establishes the required maximum-slot reservation. `evidence/timings.md` explicitly measures automated work using existing pilot material, not human construction or adjudication. Student participation is also unconfirmed. A hard cap prevents overspending, but does not establish sufficient construction yield or usable security evidence.  
   **Required change:** Complete the specified human development exercise and publish one reconciled feasibility record showing that measured construction, confirmation and independent-review work supports the fixed minimum allocation within the student, paid-work and compute ceilings.

2. **The available frame evidence does not yet support the principal comparison’s precision requirement.**  
   **Evidence:** `evidence/frame_count/README.md` identifies 17 one-function fixes among 43 examined findings, with nine supplied by Panoptic. This is a filtered pilot distribution, not the refreshed frame, so it cannot prove infeasibility. Nevertheless, the corresponding counted-spread scenario in `tools/power_results_v19.txt` gives a largest mean half-width of 0.314 and an exclusion rate of 0.38 near a true design difference of −0.33, even without unresolved outcomes. Both miss the declared requirements of 0.20 and 0.80. V20 obtains more favorable results under the earlier V distribution, but its source generates construction independently of difficulty and outcomes; it therefore does not resolve the principal risk introduced by the resource-limited constructible domain.  
   **Required change:** Produce a frame-grounded readiness analysis using the refreshed eligible-function inventory and independently validated finite-population simulations that include difficulty-dependent construction, unresolved outcomes and sparse strata, demonstrating that the retained allocation satisfies the declared RQ1a requirements after Monte Carlo uncertainty.

These are distinct gaps: the first concerns delivering the prescribed work; the second concerns whether the resulting observations can answer the principal question.

## Questions for authors

**OQ1 — Must human timings precede proposal acceptance?**

Conditional development approval is reasonable at proposal stage; final study results are not a prerequisite. However, I would require the human development record before **unqualified acceptance of this particular twelve-month research plan**. The concern is not simply that measurements are missing: the proposal’s own cost ranges put the minimum allocation close to or beyond its operating allowance, and construction losses directly threaten its scientific contribution.

The binding launch requirements are therefore an acceptable governance mechanism, but do not themselves establish C7. I would approve the development phase while withholding approval of the confirmatory plan until the feasibility record and frame-grounded precision analysis pass. Three cases need not precisely estimate throughput; they must demonstrate the complete human workflow and support conservative projections, as section 8 already requires.

Additional questions:

- How many eligible V functions per protocol does the refreshed frame actually contain after necessary-fix-scope exclusions?
- Which named, funded people will supply unexposed confirmation, duplicate construction and independent adjudication without incompatible roles?
- What observations from development will determine the conservative difficulty-dependent construction scenarios, rather than selecting scenarios because they pass the readiness criterion?
