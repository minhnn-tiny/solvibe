## Summary

The proposal studies agent-written replacements of audited Solidity functions, including functions without reported vulnerabilities, and experimentally varies faulty assertions and execution feedback. Its strongest contribution is the combination of probability sampling, executable vulnerability confirmation, and explicit accounting for unusable implementations.

The security methodology is substantially more careful than a static-warning comparison. However, the central comparison still depends on an insufficiently validated discovery process, and the flagship exploit has not yet demonstrated compliance with the proposal’s revised attribution and attacker requirements. I consider this promising but borderline for a top security venue.

## Strengths

- The threat model explicitly excludes privileged attackers, requires reachable states, and separates security violations from ordinary functional failures.
- Target-only corrections, legitimate-behavior checks, and common exploit replay provide meaningful safeguards against false vulnerability labels.
- Sampling unflagged functions addresses an important limitation of benchmarks selected exclusively from historical vulnerabilities.
- The proposal correctly limits its claims: audit silence is not safety, failed compilation is not successful avoidance, and historical replacement is not a controlled comparison of human and agent development.
- The separate-account loss artifact for finding 63008 documents victim exposure and failed withdrawals, providing stronger evidence than attacker profit alone.
- Offline execution, deployment-exposure checks, and continued embargo while known deployments remain vulnerable constitute a reasonable disclosure policy.

## Weaknesses

1. **Equal discovery budgets do not establish comparable detection sensitivity.**  
   **Evidence:** Section 5.10 uses frozen properties, optional reference implementations, and two LLM reviewers. Section 6.6 allocates approximately 150 implementations to expert validation; section 8 initially allows only 20 minutes per auditor per implementation. Historical V cases already have report-derived attacks, whereas newly introduced flaws—especially in N functions—must be discovered. Common replay helps only after an attack is found. The proposal acknowledges incomplete detection but does not specify when measured author differences become too sensitive to missed flaws to support interpretation.  
   **Required change:** Preregister a detection-validation gate using an independently investigated, author-by-stratum sample, with a quantitative sensitivity analysis that identifies the missed-vulnerability rates sufficient to reverse the primary comparison.

2. **The flagship security example has not yet passed the proposed evidentiary standard.**  
   **Evidence:** Section 5.12 explicitly says the pilot requires revised attribution and acceptance checks. The original `PoV63008_StealRewards.t.sol` ends with `assertGt(profitInEth, 0)`, which is weaker than the entitlement property required in section 4. The stronger `loss_estimate.json` records replacing the entire `TickIterator.sol` file, rather than demonstrating the specified function-body-only intervention. The supplied report also identifies dependence on another issue, H-02, and on winning a top-of-block swap when needed. These do not invalidate the attack, but leave its exact attribution and environmental requirements insufficiently demonstrated.  
   **Required change:** Produce one complete revised evidence package for 63008 that asserts victim entitlement and backing, validates a function-body-only correction, and records the capital, ordering, and interacting-bug assumptions needed by the attack.

3. **High/Medium classification lacks a common operational rule across protocols.**  
   **Evidence:** Section 4 makes High or Medium severity a condition for counting a vulnerability, while each protocol record uses “the audit platform’s severity criteria.” The proposal does not explain how newly discovered agent flaws receive comparable labels across platforms. A reverting operation could represent attacker-induced denial of service, a temporary inconvenience, or an ordinary implementation failure; these distinctions directly affect the primary endpoint.  
   **Required change:** Freeze a cross-platform severity rubric before evaluation, with explicit thresholds for financial harm and availability impact and blinded application to both historical and newly discovered findings.

4. **The security-specific novelty of the faulty-test experiment remains uncertain.**  
   **Evidence:** The supplied literature describes specification-conflicting tests and feedback interventions in [ImpossibleBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20270), and vulnerable repository tests in [EVMbench](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/wang26k.html). Section 6.4 makes injected cases confirmatory while natural cases remain descriptive. This can establish a controlled effect, but a result that agents follow incorrect executable expectations could largely reproduce existing insight in Solidity.  
   **Required change:** Preregister a security-specific mechanism analysis that traces whether faulty-test feedback causes retention of the same exploitable behavior despite contrary PoV evidence, using paired trajectories and held-out exploit outcomes.

5. **The protected minimum scope is not yet demonstrated feasible for one first-year student.**  
   **Evidence:** Section 8 retains up to 7,440 generation runs after its first four reductions, plus task construction, invariant development, candidate confirmation, attribution, common replay, and independent review. The proposal expressly acknowledges that the pilot has not measured the full labor cost. A funding gate is useful, but does not itself establish that the essential study fits twelve months.  
   **Required change:** Replace the provisional workload with a measured minimum viable study budget after the ten-case construction pilot, specifying total researcher hours, auditor hours, compute costs, and attainable precision for the retained sample.

## Ratings

C1: concern — Population sampling offers a credible distinction, but the faulty-test contribution needs a clearer security-specific insight.  
C2: pass — Exploitable financial and availability failures are consequential, and the motivation appropriately avoids attributing incidents to AI without evidence.  
C3: pass — Executable attribution, paired sampling, and controlled feedback interventions go substantially beyond applying an LLM to auditing.  
C4: concern — Comparable detection sensitivity and compliance of the pilot with the revised attribution rules remain unestablished.  
C5: concern — The general attacker model is explicit, but the flagship case still needs concrete ordering, capital, and interacting-bug assumptions.  
C6: concern — Outcomes and experimental factors are well specified, but detection validation and severity adjudication need operational decision rules.  
C7: concern — The indispensable construction and adjudication workload has not yet been shown to fit a first-year student’s twelve-month budget.  
C8: pass — Offline testing and exposure-dependent disclosure restrictions adequately address the principal stated risks.

## Questions for authors

- What unequal missed-vulnerability rates would reverse the anticipated human–agent comparison?
- Does the 63008 reward-theft scenario remain exploitable after H-02 is corrected independently?
- What observable result would distinguish the proposed security mechanism from ordinary compliance with incorrect tests?
- What is the smallest retained study that still answers RQ1 and the core PoV-repair question with useful precision?
