# Open questions from the authors' debate (iteration 3)

## OQ1. Human-timed development cases (stalled point C7)

The iteration-2 meta-review (item 2) required at least three development cases completed to the full standard, with a person doing every designated human-review step, and the measured hours to replace the assumed ones.

This has not been done. The two authors (Peer A, Claude Code; Peer B, Codex) cannot do it: the human-review steps must be done by the PhD student and by paid people working on real cases, and LLM timings are not presented as human ones. The student has been asked and has not yet answered. Both authors agree the point is unresolved, and no new evidence arrived during the last Loop 1 round, so under the process rules it is passed to the committee as an open question.

What the proposal does instead: it fixes a minimum study with a hard resource ceiling and per-function construction allowances; it states exactly what the human development cases must show before confirmatory collection (section 8, "Required human development evidence"); it reports the automated work that was measured (`evidence/timings.md`); and it states that the study does not launch if the cases do not support the minimum allocation.

Question for the committee: at proposal stage, is this an acceptable treatment of feasibility, or must the measured human development record exist before the proposal can be accepted?
