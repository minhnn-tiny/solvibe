## Summary

Version 15 makes the design difference the principal estimand of paper 1 (RQ1a). The design difference is the comparison over audit-flagged functions only, minus the domain-weighted comparison. The target population is the "constructible domain": functions whose task passes a fixed construction procedure within a fixed allowance for each function. RQ1b compares the agents with historical developer code over that domain. The fixed minimum study has these parts:
- 20 protocols, each contributing up to six V and eight N functions;
- Claude Code and Codex CLI, with two fresh attempts each;
- one audit-blind discovery campaign for each implementation;
- an executable PoV, a correction to the target body alone, and two outside adjudicators for every counted flaw.

Uncertainty comes from a delete-one-protocol jackknife. Each jackknife replicate re-estimates the domain shares. Unresolved outcomes enter through identification bounds. The repair experiment is now an unfunded extension. The proposal says plainly that it is not ready for confirmatory collection and that no human timings exist.

**What I checked.** I read these files:
- the proposal, my iteration-2 review, the rebuttal and the open questions;
- the shared data generator (`tools/power_sim_v8.py`);
- the simulation scripts and result files for V14 and V16 to V20 (`tools/power_sim_v1*.py`, `tools/power_sim_v20.py`, `tools/power_results_v1*.txt`, `tools/power_results_v20.txt`);
- the fix-scope record (`evidence/frame_count/README.md`) and the timing record (`evidence/timings.md`);
- the pilot reports.

Every number the proposal quotes from these files matches its source, and so do the pilot counts. I recomputed the development-gate probabilities (0.069, 0.455, 0.913). I also ran the authors' generator from a scratch directory outside the repository. Those results appear under weaknesses 1 to 3. I changed no repository file.

**My iteration-2 weaknesses, one line each.**
1. Adverse-bound rule missing from the power analysis. **Resolved for planning.** V17 simulates the adverse-bound decision in both directions, with unresolved rates from 0 to 0.10 that can differ by author. But V17 uses the two-V cap, not the fixed allocation. The narrower definition of "unresolved" also creates new weakness 6.
2. θ was positive under plain test obedience. **Resolved.** Each assertion type now has its own outcome: keeping the behavior its wrong assertion endorses. Both types get matched contrary evidence (6.6). The extension is outside the protected year.
3. Nothing measured sensitivity on flaws that survived an audit. **Partly resolved.** A set of 10 to 20 such flaws is specified, and using it for historical N code is labeled an assumption. How many such cases exist has not been counted (question 4).
4. A small design difference could mean weak rediscovery. **Resolved.** The reading rule requires a lower rediscovery bound of at least 0.5 and a report beside the audit-supplemented view (6.4). But the "little difference" reading cannot occur at the planned precision (new weakness 1).
5. The design-comparison precision assumed at least two V functions per protocol. **Partly resolved.** V18 and V19 now include the counted pilot distribution, which has protocols with no V function, and the claim that "about 50 V functions suffice" is withdrawn. But the only counted distribution fails the criterion, and the frame itself has not been counted (new weakness 4).
6. The detection gate was too lenient. **Resolved.** The gate passes at 5 of 8, with one revision allowed at 6 of 8 on fresh cases.
7. Reviewer and generator could share a model family. **Resolved.** A filtered view uses fuzzing plus only the reviewer from the other family, for both authors (5.7).
8. The equivalence margin was too large. **Resolved.** Paper 1 makes no equivalence claim about author security.

My iteration-2 questions:

- Questions 2 and 7 are deferred to final validation.

## Strengths

1. **The evidence is reported accurately and without overclaiming.** Each table in 6.3 and 6.4 matches its result file. The proposal reports the scenarios in which its own criterion fails: the counted pilot distribution, construction probabilities of 0.7/0.9 and 0.6/0.6, and unresolved shares of 0.02 or more.
2. **The decision rules for unresolved outcomes are now simulated.** V17 tests directional claims at the adverse bound in both directions, with asymmetric missingness. V19 evaluates the full interval that encloses both identification bounds.
3. **The domain estimator handles construction losses correctly in its variance.** The generator re-estimates the constructible shares in every jackknife replicate (`tools/power_sim_v8.py`, lines 111–113). The construction decision is made per function, before generation. A failure to supply the promised procedure is a recorded deviation, not a construction exclusion.
4. **Detection validation comes from several sources, and none is passed off as natural recall.** Seeded pairs, audit rediscovery, audit-missed flaws and auditor investigation are each reported for their own population. Any use of them for natural code is labeled an assumption (6.5).
5. **The repair extension now has a matched outcome, and it has been removed from the protected year.**
6. **The 63008 package is a complete worked example of attribution.** It has a body-only correction that keeps the empty-range behavior, fixed-state assertions, and a runner that matched all 12 expected outcomes.

## Weaknesses

1. **At the planned precision, RQ1a cannot reach its "little difference" reading, and most distortions between 0.10 and 0.30 will come out inconclusive.**
   Problem: Section 3 gives three readings for RQ1a: a large distortion, "little difference", and inconclusive. "Little difference" needs the full interval inside ±0.10, so the half-width must be below 0.10. The criterion in 6.4 allows a mean half-width of 0.20, and the simulated half-widths run from 0.16 to 0.31. The power target sits at a true difference of −0.33, more than three times the meaningful threshold of 0.10.
   Evidence: I ran the V19 setting: 20 protocols, V cap 6, two attempts, multiplier 1.16, all tasks built, no unresolved outcomes, 12 scenarios with 300 studies each.

   | True design difference | Mean full-interval half-width | Share inside ±0.10 | Share wholly below −0.10 |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | +0.068 | 0.16 | 0.00 | 0.00 |
   | −0.030 | 0.17 | 0.00 | 0.00 |
   | −0.104 | 0.16 | 0.00 | 0.01 |
   | −0.176 | 0.16 | 0.00 | 0.11–0.13 |
   | −0.262 | 0.16 | 0.00 | 0.51–0.53 |
   | −0.328 | 0.16 | 0.00 | 0.85–0.87 |

   No simulated study, in any scenario, produced "little difference". In practice, the fixed minimum can only report a large distortion or say the result is inconclusive.
   Required change: In sections 3 and 6.4, state the smallest distortion that the fixed minimum detects with probability 0.80. For each planning scenario, give the simulated share of each of the three readings. Drop the "little difference" reading unless some allocation gives it a real chance when the true difference is near zero.

2. **On the D endpoint, much of the design difference in the planning scenarios comes from agents producing code that does not work, not from security.**
   Problem: Within a stratum, write the agent D rate as w_A·v_A, where w is the share of working outputs and v is the detected-vulnerability rate among working outputs. The historical rate is w_H·v_H. Then d = w_A(v_A − v_H) + (w_A − w_H)·v_H. The second term is not zero whenever agents fail more often than historical code. It is large in stratum V, because v_H is high there. Section 3 itself says "A lower vulnerability count caused by failed generation is not evidence of better security." Yet RQ1a applies the 0.10 threshold to the whole difference on D.
   Evidence: In the generator (`tools/power_sim_v8.py`, lines 52, 89 and 97–101), agents work in 72% of attempts, vulnerability is independent of working, and historical code always works. Take the scenario with agent V rate 0.9, equal recall and h_N = 0.02. There, working agent outputs carry the V flaw exactly as often as historical code. Yet the true design difference is −0.176 (my run). That is (0.72 − 1) × 0.63, a meaningful distortion with no security difference at all. In the scenario that sets the −0.33 target, 0.176 of the 0.328 comes from this term.
   The pilot shows the same effect: in two runs that rebuilt the 63008 bug, Aider "wrote the bug but broke the function" (`docs/pilot/pilot_results.md`, item 3). The reverse can also happen. If a blind-built ordinary check catches the historical flaw, historical D in stratum V drops to zero, and the generator has no historical functional failure. SusVibes reports security among functionally correct solutions for this reason (https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.03262).
   Required change: Pre-specify two things:
   - RQ1a on D is always reported with this two-term split for each stratum;
   - the meaningful-distortion reading applies to the design difference of the first term, which is security among working outputs; the second term is reported as the part caused by functional failure.

3. **At the optimistic end of its own stated rates, the fixed minimum breaks its own reservation rule for each protocol. The construction allowance that remains is not linked to the construction probabilities in V20.**
   Problem: Section 8 requires h_setup + 14·h_build + 56·h_generation + 70·h_campaign + h_secondary ≤ 45 hours for the maximum allocation. Builders receive the strata labels sealed (5.4), so one allowance must serve both V and N functions. A different allowance for each stratum would reveal the label.
   Evidence: Take the optimistic end of each section 8 rate:
   - preparation 6 hours;
   - campaign operation 0.1 hours;
   - confirmation 0.5 hours per candidate, at the lower pilot candidate rate of 8 in 64.

   The 70 campaigns then need about 11.4 hours. That leaves h_build ≤ (45 − 6 − 11.4)/14 ≈ 2.0 hours, before any generation, secondary work or reserve for the declared candidate limit. The stated construction rates are 4–8 hours for V functions and 1.5–3 hours for N functions. Even with separate optimistic rates for each stratum, the maximum allocation needs about 53 hours. At the pessimistic rates, preparation and campaigns alone need about 50 hours. The proposal's figures of 43 to 100 hours are averages over expected slots, and section 8 itself says averages are not enough.
   An allowance near two hours would leave few V functions constructible. V20 instead assumes construction probabilities from 0.6 to 0.9. I reran the V20 setting with a V construction probability of 0.4 (N 0.9). The mean full-interval half-widths were 0.22 to 0.26. The rate of excluding −0.10 near a true difference of −0.34 was 0.49 to 0.55.
   Required change: Find an allocation (number of protocols, V cap, N functions per protocol) whose reservation for the maximum allocation fits 45 hours at the stated rates, using one common allowance at least as long as the stated V construction time. Rerun V19 and V20 for that allocation, with a V construction probability that the allowance justifies. If no allocation both fits and meets the RQ1a criterion, say so in section 8.

4. **The V distribution that decides whether RQ1a can pass has not been measured, though it could be measured now without the student.**
   Problem: V19 shows that the fixed minimum passes under the assumed distribution and fails under the counted one:
   - assumed distribution (2 + Poisson(2), never fewer than two V functions): half-width 0.167, exclusion rate 0.83;
   - counted pilot distribution: half-width 0.314, exclusion rate 0.38.

   Neither describes the frame. The counted vector comes from the 43 filtered pilot findings, not from every High and Medium finding of those protocols. Ammplify has 36 such findings and only one is among the 43. stETH by EaseDeFi has 12, and one is among the 43 (`evidence/frame_count/README.md`, Result 2). So the counted vector probably understates V counts, and the assumed one has no data behind it.
   Evidence: section 6.4; the V19 results (`tools/power_results_v19.txt`). Classifying the 43 findings took 864 seconds of automated work (`evidence/timings.md`).
   Required change: Before acceptance, take these steps:
   - draw 20 to 30 protocols at random from the contest-listing frame;
   - classify the fix scope of every qualifying High and Medium finding, as in `evidence/frame_count/`;
   - report the V counts per protocol after the multi-function exclusion;
   - rerun V19 and V20 at the fixed allocation with that distribution.

5. **The decision to launch depends on which scenarios are "retained", and no rule fixes that choice.**
   Problem: Section 6.4 requires the width and exclusion criteria to hold "for every retained planning scenario". Section 8 makes launch depend on it. Every scenario simulated so far that is not optimistic fails at least one requirement:
   - the counted pilot distribution: half-width 0.314, exclusion 0.38;
   - construction probabilities 0.7 and 0.9: half-width 0.196, exclusion 0.71;
   - construction probabilities 0.6 and 0.6: half-width 0.211, exclusion 0.64;
   - unresolved share 0.02: exclusion 0.69.

   So the outcome depends on which scenarios are retained. Section 8 itself says three development cases cannot estimate construction yield or unresolved rates. There are two further gaps:
   - V20 chooses a different multiplier for each construction scenario (1.08 to 1.14), but the study must fix its multiplier before it knows its construction rate.
   - RQ1b at the fixed allocation is evaluated only with uncalibrated plain intervals, whose coverage is 0.925 to 0.935.

   Evidence: sections 6.3, 6.4 and 8; the V20 results (`tools/power_results_v20.txt`).
   Required change: Pre-specify how development and frame measurements become retained scenarios. For example:
   - use the measured V distribution from weakness 4;
   - set the V construction probability at the lower 80% confidence limit of development yields;
   - set unresolved shares at the upper 80% limit, with a stated floor.

   Also require one RQ1a multiplier and one RQ1b Holm level, each calibrated across all retained scenarios.

6. **Candidates left unchecked at the confirmation limit now count as "not confirmed". This takes a loss that can differ by author out of the bounds.**
   Problem: In iteration 2, candidates beyond the confirmation limit were unresolved. Section 5.2 now says "A candidate not confirmed when those budgets end is 'not confirmed.'" This lowers the unresolved rate and helps power. But the limit is the same for every implementation, while campaigns differ in how many candidates they produce. Suppose one author's code draws more reviewer flags. Then the limit cuts off more of that author's real flaws. That is a difference in sensitivity caused by the procedure, and the identification bounds no longer show it.
   Evidence: sections 5.2, 5.7 and 6.2.
   Required change: For each implementation, record whether the candidate limit cut its list. Report that share by author and stratum. Add a secondary bound that treats implementations with a cut list as unresolved.

## Ratings

- C1: concern — The principal contribution is the size of a sampling effect. Its sign follows from choosing functions because the historical code failed, and on D much of its size comes from agent functional failure (weaknesses 1 and 2).
- C2: pass — Contracts hold funds, Solidity developers report daily AI use, and the proposal claims no rate of AI-caused losses.
- C3: pass — Audit-blind discovery for each implementation, body-only attribution, domain estimation with estimated shares, and inference from bounds go well beyond applying an LLM to a task.
- C4: concern — RQ1a on D counts failed generation as part of the distortion, against the proposal's own rule, and the new meaning of "not confirmed" hides a sensitivity difference caused by the procedure (weaknesses 2 and 6).
- C5: pass — The attacker, the environment, the assumptions for each protocol, the severity rubric and the exclusions are all explicit.
- C6: concern — RQs, endpoints, baselines, validation and threats are specified. But the principal criterion passes only under an unmeasured V distribution and optimistic construction, "little difference" cannot occur, and the readiness rule leaves the key choices open (weaknesses 1, 4 and 5).
- C7: fail — Even at the optimistic end of its own stated rates, the maximum allocation needs about 53 hours against a 45-hour cap for each protocol, and no human timing exists (weakness 3).
- C8: pass — Attacks run offline, new findings go through the protocol's security contact with a 90-day re-check and an embargo, and seeds stay local and are marked.

## Questions for authors

1. V functions have public fixes, and N functions do not. A model that saw the fixed code can avoid the V flaw. That pushes the design difference in the very direction RQ1a measures. How many frame protocols have fix commits dated after the documented cutoffs of current Claude and GPT models? If there are enough, would you make that subset the primary frame?
2. In the eight pilot tasks, would an ordinary functional suite written from documentation fail the historical V implementation? Before generation and without audit access, who decides whether a check that catches the flaw is an ordinary check or a security check?
3. What is the candidate limit for each campaign, and how much of h_campaign is reserved for it?
4. The fix-scope record lists earlier audits of several pilot protocols: five for Panoptic, two for Sorella, one for YuzuUSD and two for Megapot. Have you checked whether any later finding concerns code inside an earlier audit's scope? That would give a first count for the audit-missed set.
5. Natural investigation allows up to four first assessments per cell. Which quantity, by author and by stratum, can four assessments estimate, and with what expected interval?
6. Under the counted distribution, how often is a stratum empty in a jackknife replicate, and which treatment of that case will you validate?

## Open questions

**OQ1: may the proposal be accepted before human-timed development cases exist?** Yes, on two conditions. The authors cannot produce human timings, and the proposal already makes the three cases a binding month-one gate. Demanding them before acceptance would make the requirement impossible to meet by design. But conditional acceptance needs a plan that is consistent with its own assumptions, and this one is not. At the optimistic stated rates, the reservation for the maximum allocation already exceeds the 45-hour cap (weakness 3). The month-two launch decision also needs a fixed rule, or it becomes a judgment call (weakness 5). Once weaknesses 3 and 5 are fixed, the human cases should remain a launch condition, not an acceptance condition. I would also suggest reviewing a smaller second allocation now. Then a failed month-two check would lead to a reviewed plan instead of a lost year.

## Overall score

The revision does what I asked in iteration 2. V17 runs the adverse-bound rule in the power analysis, θ now has a matched outcome, and the audit-missed set is specified. Its reporting remains unusually careful. But making RQ1a the principal estimand exposes three new problems:
- the estimand on D mixes functional failure with security;
- its precision can only detect distortions of about 0.3 or more;
- the fixed minimum does not fit its own budget rule at the authors' own optimistic rates.

Three changes would raise my score to 4:
- the split of the D-based design difference into a security part and a functional-failure part (weakness 2);
- an allocation that fits the reservation rule, with V20 rerun at a matching construction probability (weakness 3);
- a measured V distribution, with V19 and V20 rerun on it (weakness 4).
