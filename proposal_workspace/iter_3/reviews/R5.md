## Summary

The proposal plans an empirical study of AI agents that rewrite single function bodies in audited Solidity protocols. It draws a probability sample of functions from about 20 protocols. Functions linked to qualifying audit findings form stratum V. All other frame functions form stratum N. For each sampled function, a builder who has not seen the audit writes a request and security properties. Claude Code and Codex CLI each write the body twice. The historical developer body is the baseline.

A flaw counts only when four things hold. The implementation's own audit-blind campaign (Foundry fuzzing plus two LLM reviewers) finds it. A PoV shows it executing. A change to that function body alone removes it. Two outside adjudicators agree. The principal quantity (RQ1a) is how much the agent-minus-developer difference changes when the analysis uses only flagged functions instead of all functions that could be built under a fixed procedure. Validation uses seeded flaws, rediscovery of audit findings, budgeted auditor review and flaws missed by an earlier audit. A repair extension on faulty test assertions is specified, but it gets no student hours in year one.

Feasibility rests on a 1,800-hour student ceiling, a 45-hour cap per protocol and paid adjudicators. No person has yet timed any development case. The proposal says that collection will not start unless development evidence meets stated requirements. It is careful and candid about its own limits. My concerns are that its own numbers point toward not launching, and that the principal result is largely set by how the strata are defined.

## Strengths

- The evidence standard for a counted flaw is strong and clearly specified. It requires an executable violation, a correction confined to the function body, preserved legitimate behaviour and two independent decisions. The three PoV outcomes (violation observed, checked and absent, unresolved) avoid treating exit codes as verdicts.
- The 63008 worked example is reproducible and matches its logs. The version-2 summary log (`evidence/63008/logs/v2_summary.log`) shows all 12 expected outcomes, including 56 pass and 4 fail on the original suite after the correction.
- Primary labels come only from each implementation's own audit-blind campaign. Audit-assisted and pooled replay views are kept separate and secondary. The staffing rules for exposed and unexposed people are concrete.
- Including unflagged functions under a declared sampling design targets a real gap. The literature review (section 6, G1) finds no verified study that compares agents with the original developer on the same code, with a control group of correctly written code, judged by the same executable exploit.
- Failed generation stays in the denominator. Compile-only (K) and working-without-flaw outcomes are reported, so fewer detected flaws caused by broken code is not presented as better security.
- The simulation code and results are available. The text states what each simulation does not validate, and it states a no-launch rule instead of a fallback that quietly changes the contribution.
- The threat model, severity rubric and disclosure plan are explicit.

## Weaknesses

1. **The only frame evidence based on real data fails the principal precision criterion.** Every passing planning result (V18, the V19 "earlier assumption" rows, V20) draws 2 + Poisson(2) V functions per protocol. Under that draw, every protocol has at least two V functions, and about 3.9 are sampled on average under the six-V cap. The fix-scope record for the 43 pilot findings (`evidence/frame_count/README.md`) gives one-function V counts of [9, 2, 2, 0, 1, 1, 1, 1, 0, 0]. Three of ten protocols have none, and the cap yields about 1.4 V functions per protocol. On that spread, the V19 results (`tools/power_results_v19.txt`) give a largest mean full-interval half-width of 0.314 with zero unresolved outcomes, against the 0.20 target. The interval excludes −0.10 near a true value of −0.33 in only 0.38 of studies. The proposal says the counted spread is filtered and is not a lower bound. That is fair, but no evidence supports the planning distribution either.
   Required change: Before acceptance, count one-function V functions per protocol for a random sample of protocols from the refreshed, listing-based frame. Rerun the V19/V20 full-interval check on that distribution. Then present a fixed allocation that meets the 0.20 criterion under it, for example more protocols, or protocol sampling in proportion to V count. The 43-finding classification took 864 seconds of automated work, so this count is cheap.

2. **At the proposal's own optimistic unit costs, the maximum-slot reservation does not fit in 45 hours.** Section 8 requires that setup + 14·h_build + 56·h_generation + 70·h_campaign + h_secondary ≤ 45 for the full six-V, eight-N allocation. Take the lowest assumed rates: setup 6 h, V construction 4 h, N construction 1.5 h, campaign operation 0.1 h, and confirmation 0.5 h per candidate at the 8/64 candidate rate. The total is 6 + 24 + 12 + 7 + 4.4 ≈ 53 hours, before any generation or secondary work. If h_build is one value for both strata, as the inequality writes it, the 14 × 4 = 56 construction hours alone exceed the cap. The text reports average costs of 43 to 100 hours but never the maximum-slot figure it says must fit.
   Required change: Evaluate the maximum-slot inequality at the low, middle and high assumed rates, with separate V and N construction allowances. Then change the fixed allocation (N per protocol, the V cap or the number of protocols) or the hour ceiling so the inequality holds at the middle rates.

3. **There is no evidence that the audit-blind campaign can rediscover audit-grade logic flaws.** The pilot's LLM security reviews were not audit-blind. The pilot process log (`docs/pilot/process_log.md`, step 18) states that the prompt "holds the requirement, the target file … and the five High and Medium findings of the audit report." The reading rule in section 6.4 needs a lower 95% rediscovery bound of at least 0.5. The development gate passes with probability 0.455 at recall 0.5 (table in section 5.9). The verified literature gives reasons for doubt. LLM auditors over-report on real contracts (Khalid et al., ESEM 2026, https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ESEM.2026.45). No agent completed an end-to-end exploit on 22 incidents dated after the models' release (Peng et al., Re-Evaluating EVMBench, https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10795).
   Required change: Now, run the frozen LLM-reviewer step without audit findings on the historical bodies of the 17 one-function pilot findings. Report how many each reviewer flags at candidate level, with an interval. State which of these cases then leave the gate reserve. This needs only model calls, and it gives an upper bound on confirmed rediscovery.

4. **Agents' functional failures feed directly into the principal effect.** D = C·L·V_own. Historical V code compiles and passes by assumption, and it carries the flagged flaw. Agent D is capped by the compile-and-pass rate, which is 0.9 × 0.8 = 0.72 in the planning generator (`tools/power_sim_v8.py`). Suppose agents' working code were vulnerable as often as the developer's (0.9) and detection were equal at 0.7. Then d_V = (0.72 × 0.9 − 0.9) × 0.7 ≈ −0.18, and G ≈ −0.18, with no security difference among working implementations. Section 6.2 reports the working-without-flaw outcome separately, but RQ1a is defined on D alone.
   Required change: Pre-specify that RQ1a is also estimated on the vulnerability rate among implementations that compile and pass the functional checks. State that a negative G explained by functional failure will not be described as a sampling effect on security.

5. **The "little difference" outcome cannot be reached at the planned precision.** Section 3 calls a result "little difference" only when the full interval lies inside ±0.10, which needs a half-width below 0.10. The precision target is a mean half-width of at most 0.20. Every simulated setting gives 0.163 to 0.394 (`tools/power_results_v19.txt`, `tools/power_results_v20.txt`). In practice, the study can only conclude that the flagged-only view differs by more than 0.10, or that the result is inconclusive.
   Required change: Restate RQ1a as a one-sided question: does restricting to flagged functions make the agents look better by more than 0.10? Remove the "little difference" outcome unless a design with an expected half-width below 0.10 is shown.

6. **The principal result is largely set in advance, and it mostly reflects how the developer's code was selected.** V is defined by audit findings in the historical code, so historical D in V roughly equals the campaign's rediscovery rate. V functions are a small share of the domain: the generator draws about 4 V against 30 to 120 N functions per protocol. So the domain estimate is close to the N comparison, and G = (1 − π_VB)(d_V − d_N) is close to d_V − d_N. The proposal admits the sign is predictable (section 3). The idea that samples chosen because the human failed cannot give a general rate is already known. Coston and Kennedy discuss it for case-control designs (https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09016), and the literature review applies it to Asare et al. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.04741). The part no verified work measures is the unflagged comparison judged by executable exploits (literature review, G1). V20 estimates it with a raw half-width near 0.05, against 0.16 or more for G. Its plain t-interval coverage is 0.925 to 0.935, so it still needs calibration.
   Required change: Make the domain-weighted agent-minus-developer difference (RQ1b) a co-principal estimand, with its own calibrated interval and precision target. Present G as a contrast derived from the two stratum differences.

7. **Training-data exposure is likely and only partly checked.** The pilot audits date from September 2025 to January 2026 (fix-scope record). Models "current at the freeze" will probably have documented cutoffs after those dates [uncertain for any specific model], so the post-cutoff subset in section 6.8 may be empty. The recall flag records only reproduction of distinctive fix lines. Reproduction of the historical body itself is not flagged, although it would pull agent results toward the developer's in both strata. Al-Kaswan et al. (MSR 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10496) report that models reproduce buggy lines they have seen.
   Required change: Extend the pre-specified recall flag to reproduction of the historical body in both V and N, with a similarity threshold fixed before generation. Report RQ1a and RQ1b with flagged cases removed.

8. **Request wording can swamp the effect, and that variation is not in the uncertainty.** In the pilot, 13 of 13 compiled runs reproduced the bug from requests that described the vulnerable behaviour, against 3 of 15 from requests that stated the corrected rule (section 6.4). In the main study, one blind builder writes each request. In the duplicate exercise, the second request "cannot rescue" a failed construction and is never used for generation (section 5.4).
   Required change: For the 15 functions that receive two independent requests, run generation and the primary campaign on both requests. Report how much D changes between requests, and include that variation in the RQ1a uncertainty statement.

9. **The rule for unresolved outcomes makes readiness almost impossible to show.** In V19, under the favourable frame, the half-width rises from 0.167 with no unresolved outcomes to 0.203 at 2% and 0.253 at 5%. Exclusion of −0.10 near −0.33 falls from 0.83 to 0.35. Readiness requires the criterion to hold under the unresolved scenarios that are kept (section 8), but the proposal does not say which scenarios are kept. If development sees zero unresolved cases in n evaluations, the 95% upper limit is about 3/n. That needs about 150 evaluations just to reach 2%, and the criterion already fails at 2%.
   Required change: State now which unresolved shares the readiness check must cover and the rule that derives them from development data. If that rule can require 2% or more, move the worst-case bounds to a sensitivity analysis behind a primary analysis with a stated missingness assumption.

10. **The target population is set by the budget.** Section 6.8 says that "changing the allowance changes the target population." The allowance is chosen to fit the 45-hour cap. V construction is assumed to take 4 to 8 hours against 1.5 to 3 for N, so harder V functions are more likely to fail construction. That shrinks π_VB and measures d_V on the easier part of V. In V20, construction probabilities of 0.6 in both strata raise the half-width to 0.211 even with construction independent of outcomes.
    Required change: Pre-specify a minimum construction yield per stratum (for example 0.7). Below it, RQ1a and RQ1b are reported only as descriptive results for the constructed tasks, not as estimates for the frame.

11. **It is hard to find which numbers support the current plan.** The proposal runs to about 1,200 lines. Sections 6.3 and 6.4 present four simulation versions (V17 to V20), including allocations that were dropped (15 protocols, V cap 4), and each version comes with a list of what it does not validate.
    Required change: Replace the simulation history in sections 6.3 and 6.4 with one table for the current allocation (V and N distribution, unresolved and construction scenarios, half-width, coverage, exclusion rate). Move earlier versions to an appendix.

## Ratings

- C1: concern — Failure-selected benchmarks are already known to bias human-versus-AI comparisons, so what remains is a Solidity measurement whose sign is known in advance.
- C2: pass — Solidity developers report heavy daily AI use, and exploitable logic flaws in audited code are a documented problem, although direct incident evidence linking AI code to losses is thin.
- C3: pass — Audit-blind discovery for each implementation, attribution confined to the function body, and survey weights with identification bounds go well beyond applying an LLM to a task.
- C4: concern — Functional failures feed into the principal endpoint, the "little difference" conclusion cannot be reached, and detection has no audit-blind evidence.
- C5: pass — Attacker capital, ordering, oracle assumptions, severity rules and attribution are explicit, and all execution is offline.
- C6: concern — The RQs, metrics, validation arms and threats are named, but the only real-data frame fails the precision criterion and the validation intervals are wide.
- C7: fail — At the proposal's own optimistic rates the maximum-slot reservation exceeds the 45-hour cap, no human time has been measured, and the plan assumes no coursework.
- C8: pass — Offline execution, disclosure through security contacts, a 90-day re-check, embargo of exploit details and marking of seeded flaws are all specified.

## Questions for authors

1. How many N frame functions do you expect per protocol, and what value of π_VB does that imply? This decides whether the domain estimate is in practice the N comparison.
2. Which tests make up the frozen functional suite L? In 63008, four repository tests assert the old starting-boundary behaviour, and a correct body fails them. If repository tests are in L, how are such tests identified before anyone sees the audit?
3. Is h_build one allowance for both strata, or separate V and N allowances?
4. Can you give an order-of-magnitude compute cost per protocol from the pilot runs? The pilot log records $6.83 for a single Codex + Sonnet 4.5 repair run.
5. With only two primary configurations, what does "agent ordering and its uncertainty" add beyond two separate comparisons with the developer?
6. Why is the repair extension part of this proposal if it has no year-one hours? Would it be cleaner as a separate proposal?

**Answer to OQ1 (human-timed development cases).** In principle, a proposal does not need measured human timings before it can be accepted. A conditional plan with binding launch requirements is normal at this stage. Here, though, the proposal's own arithmetic already points against launch. The maximum-slot reservation fails at the optimistic assumed rates (weakness 2). The only real-data V distribution fails the precision criterion (weakness 1). So a conditional acceptance would very likely end in a "feasibility and methods record," which section 8 itself says is not the proposed contribution.

My position is that the full three-case record should stay a launch requirement, not an acceptance requirement. Acceptance should, however, require two things. First, a budget whose maximum-slot inequality holds at the middle assumed rates. Second, at least one development case on a pilot protocol, timed with the student doing every designated human step and including a probability-selected set of N functions. That single case tests the largest unknown, which is blind V construction time. It costs weeks, not months. I agree that LLM timings cannot stand in for it.

## Overall score

The design is rigorous and honest. But in its current form the proposal's own evidence suggests it will not launch, and the principal result is largely determined by how V is defined. Fixing weaknesses 1, 2 and 3 would raise my score to 3. Also addressing weaknesses 4, 5 and 6 would raise it to 4.

Score: 2
